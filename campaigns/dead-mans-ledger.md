# Dead Man's Ledger

## Pitch

The Sorrel Flyer pulls out of Bitterroot Flats at noon, bound across the desert for Paloma Junction, carrying cattle money, a chained outlaw, a preacher who won't stop praying, and a railroad baron who never lets go of a black calfskin book. They say every name written in that book comes due, to the minute. By midnight the train will cross the trestle over Coldwater Canyon, and somebody aboard owes more than they can pay.

## DM Truths

1. 1879, the Sorrel Territory. The Sorrel and Pacific Railway builds west and gets land cheap, because people who refuse to sell keep dying on schedule.
2. The Ledger is a black calfskin account book, 200 pages, half full, once owned by Silas Crane, a bookkeeper hanged for embezzlement in 1851, who swore on the gallows: "Every debt in my book will be paid." Each entry has three parts: a name, a debt, and an hour. If the debt is paid before the hour, the line strikes itself through in red. If not, at the hour the debtor dies by "accident": a fall, a spooked horse, a stopped heart. Deaths are told briefly and offstage.
3. The writer of an entry must be the Ledger's keeper. Every collected debt is added to the keeper's own line on the last page. When the keeper's line is full, the Ledger collects the keeper. That line now reads: "Ambrose Kettridge, Keeper. Owes: forty-one lives taken by his hand. Due: midnight, the 14th of October, at the Coldwater trestle." Today is the 14th.
4. Ambrose Kettridge, the railroad's president, has two ways out. Pass the keepership: anyone who willingly signs the Ledger's keeper line becomes keeper and inherits the whole debt. He has pasted a fake freight receipt over the keeper page ("Received one ledger, in good order") and plans to get someone to sign it before midnight. Or close the book, which he does not know how to do.
5. The Ledger closes forever when its keeper writes under their own signature the answer to Silas Crane's riddle on the first page, and means it. The riddle: "I am heavier the longer you hold me and lighter than air when let go. Name me in ink, keeper, and my jaws are shut." The answer is "forgiven" (also accept "forgiveness" or "I forgive"). When written and meant, every unpaid debt in the book is struck out, and the keeper's line too. A keeper who writes it without meaning it (Kettridge, unless broken) sees the ink run off the page.
6. Reverend Elijah Stroud was the keeper before Kettridge. Nine years ago, Kettridge, then a land agent, signed what Stroud told him was a church donation receipt. He has regretted it daily and boarded to burn the Ledger. It will not burn, cut or drown. He does not know the riddle's answer, but he knows the rule that the keeper must write it.
7. Mercy Dorrance leads the Dorrance Gang (five riders). Her brother Hollis is chained in the mail car, being taken to hang in Paloma for robbery. Hollis is also in the Ledger: "Hollis Dorrance. Owes: the deed to Ash Creek ranch. Due: four o'clock, the 14th." The gang plans to rob the train at the Ash Creek water stop at 5:00. They do not know about the 4:00 deadline.
8. Other entries due during the trip: "Wendell Plum (the conductor). Owes: his silence about the Bitterroot ledger books. Due: seven o'clock." (He can pay by burning his own copy of the railroad's real accounts, which he hid in his cap.) "Rosalind Achebe (rancher, passenger). Owes: water rights to Sweetgrass Spring. Due: ten o'clock." (She can pay by signing the rights over, or by the book closing.)
9. Kettridge's bodyguard is Lucius Hobb, a Blackwell Agency detective with a scarred jaw. Hobb does not know the Ledger's secret; he thinks his boss is superstitious.
10. Mercy Dorrance has a backup plan: if the robbery fails, her gang will dynamite the Coldwater trestle at midnight and drop Kettridge's private car into the canyon. They will set the charges at 11:00.

The train, front to back: engine (engineer Bartholomew "Smoke" Iwu, fireman Dot Garroway), tender, mail car (Hollis in chains, the express safe), baggage car, first passenger car, dining car, second passenger car, Kettridge's private car the Gilded Hen. The Ledger stays in Kettridge's breast pocket or the Gilded Hen's iron strongbox.

The schedule: noon leave Bitterroot Flats. 2:00 Sweetgrass stop (5 minutes). 4:00 Hollis's hour. 5:00 Ash Creek water stop (20 minutes). 7:00 Plum's hour. Sunset 7:15. 10:00 Rosalind's hour. 11:00 Mercy's men set charges. Midnight Coldwater trestle, Kettridge's hour. 2:00 Paloma Junction.

## Table Notes

Dusty, wry, spooky; gunfights end fast and talking is always an option. The Ledger never acts on its own; it only keeps its word. Show deaths by their aftermath and a hat in the wind; never in detail. Pace: Act 1 departure (45 min), Act 2 Ash Creek (60), Act 3 night train (60), Act 4 the trestle (45).

Recurring details: the clack of rails, slowing on curves. Kettridge's pocket watch, which he opens and snaps shut. The smell of coal smoke and sage. When a debt comes due, every clock and watch on the train ticks once, loudly, together.

## Characters

- **Josephine "Jo" Harlan**, bounty hunter escorting Hollis Dorrance. Might +2, Agility +2, Wits +1, Spirit +0. HP 14. Gear: Winchester (d8), knife (d6), key to Hollis's chains. Hook: she gets 500 dollars for Hollis alive in Paloma. She rode with Mercy once, years ago, and owes her a life.
- **Abner Fitch**, cardsharp. Might +0, Agility +2, Wits +2, Spirit +1. HP 12. Gear: derringer (d6, two shots), marked deck, 140 dollars. Hook: Kettridge holds Abner's IOU for 3,000 dollars, and promised to tear it up if Abner signs "a little receipt" before midnight.
- **Mateo Salazar**, railroad surveyor. Might +1, Agility +1, Wits +3, Spirit +0. HP 12. Gear: surveyor's transit, Colt revolver (d6), field books. Hook: his surveys picked the land the railroad wanted. He has noticed the owners of every parcel died on the day they refused to sell. His father, Ignacio Salazar, refused last week.
- **Winifred "Winnie" Cartwright**, railway telegrapher riding to her new post. Might +0, Agility +1, Wits +2, Spirit +2. HP 11. Gear: portable telegraph key (can tap the line at any stop), shotgun (d8, two shots). Hook: last month she relayed a Kettridge telegram that said "Write the Achebe woman for the tenth hour." She did not understand it then.
- **Ruth Ellery**, express company guard for the mail car safe. Might +3, Agility +0, Wits +0, Spirit +2. HP 16. Gear: coach gun (d8), brass knuckles (d6), safe key. Hook: Ruth rode with the Dorrance Gang before going straight; Mercy will recognize her and Hollis calls her "Ruthie".

## Opening Scene

Noon at Bitterroot Flats. The Sorrel Flyer hisses at the platform, steam rolling over the heroes' boots. Ambrose Kettridge, tall and silver-haired in dove gray, watches the boarding from the last car, pocket watch in hand. In the mail car, Hollis Dorrance, 22, sits in chains and sings a hymn badly. A preacher in a dusty black coat, Reverend Stroud, boards without a ticket and stares at Kettridge's breast pocket. The whistle blows. As the train pulls out, every watch aboard ticks once, all together, loud enough to hear. Ask each player who they are and where on the train they sit, then let them roam.

## Acts

### Act 1: Clocks on the Rails

**Goal:** learn what the Ledger is, and try to save Hollis before 4:00.

**Scenes:**
- **The Gilded Hen.** Red velvet, a brass spittoon, an iron strongbox, rye. Kettridge offers visitors money, jobs or debt relief. He asks each hero to "witness a freight receipt" before midnight, smiling. Hobb stands by the door.
- **The Preacher.** Reverend Stroud, in the second passenger car, will speak to anyone who asks about the book. He explains the rules from DM Truths 2 and 3 but hides that he was its keeper. Spirit DC 13, or showing him a death the Ledger caused, gets the confession: "I was its keeper. I tricked him into signing. God forgive me, I would do it again to be free of it."
- **Hollis in the Mail Car.** Hollis refused to sell Ash Creek ranch to the railroad. Told he is in the Ledger, he laughs, then goes quiet. Paying his debt: he must sign the Ash Creek deed over to the Sorrel and Pacific before 4:00. Kettridge has the deed form ready in the Gilded Hen and will hand it over gladly. Or the heroes can steal the Ledger and read the entry.
- **Sweetgrass Stop.** 2:00, five minutes. Winnie can tap the telegraph (Wits DC 10) and learn from the Paloma office: "Six parcel owners deceased this month, all on schedule. Kettridge Ledger rumor among crews." Mateo can see his father's ranch from the line, and a black wreath on the gate: his father died yesterday at noon.
- **Four O'Clock.** If Hollis has not signed by 4:00, every watch ticks together, and the chains on his wrists jerk tight and the mail car door bangs open in a gust. Ruth or Jo can save him from falling from the train (Agility DC 16, or DC 13 if they were already holding him); if nobody does, he falls and the gang will be out for blood at Ash Creek.

**Clues:**
- The Ledger's rules: from Stroud, or by reading the book (Wits DC 13 to steal it from Kettridge's pocket; Abner gets +2).
- Kettridge's own line, due at midnight at the trestle: reading the last page, or Stroud, or Kettridge muttering "midnight" every time he checks his watch (Wits DC 13 to notice).
- Debts can be paid to strike an entry: Stroud, or watching Hollis's line turn red if he signs.

**Ends when:** 4:00 passes and the train nears Ash Creek.

### Act 2: The Ash Creek Water Stop

**Goal:** survive, prevent or join the robbery.

**Scenes:**
- **The Water Tower.** 5:00. A water tank on stilts, a pump shack, cottonwoods, a dry creek. The train stops for 20 minutes. Mercy Dorrance and four riders are hidden in the cottonwoods.
- **The Robbery.** The gang rides out shooting into the air. Mercy wants Hollis, and the Ledger (to blackmail the railroad). The heroes can fight, parley, or bargain. Mercy recognizes Ruth and Jo; with either present, parley is Spirit DC 10. If Hollis is alive and handed over, the gang leaves the train alone and switches to the trestle plan. If Hollis fell at 4:00, Mercy wants Kettridge dead, and nothing short of that will satisfy her.
- **Conductor Plum.** In the confusion, Conductor Wendell Plum confides in a kind-seeming hero: the page in his cap, from Kettridge's real accounts, proves the railroad paid for "accidents". If he is told he is in the Ledger for 7:00, he can pay his debt by burning the page; or he can keep it as evidence and gamble that someone saves him from whatever accident comes at 7:00 (a choice between sure safety and proof).
- **Hobb's Orders.** Hobb is told to keep the heroes out of the Gilded Hen. If they read the Ledger, Kettridge writes one hero's name with a debt from their hook, due at midnight: for Abner, "owes 3,000 dollars"; for Mateo, "owes his silence"; for the others, "owes the Sorrel and Pacific their signature". Paying is signing the false receipt.

**Clues:**
- The gang's trestle plan: from Mercy if parleyed, or a captured rider (Seth Dorrance, cousin, 16, talks if offered water), or Winnie overhearing at the pump shack.
- Proof the railroad killed for land: Plum's page, or Mateo's surveys matched to Winnie's telegram dates (Wits DC 13).

**Ends when:** the train pulls out of Ash Creek with or without the gang, at about 5:20.

### Act 3: The Night Train

**Goal:** solve the riddle, deal with Kettridge, and decide who should hold the Ledger.

**Scenes:**
- **Seven O'Clock.** Plum's hour. If unpaid, every watch ticks together, and Plum steps between cars and slips; a hero can catch him (Agility DC 13). If caught, he lives, because the Ledger is satisfied by an accident it caused, not a death (Stroud did not know this; it is the one mercy in the book: a debtor rescued from their accident is free).
- **The First Page.** Silas Crane's riddle: "I am heavier the longer you hold me and lighter than air when let go. Name me in ink, keeper, and my jaws are shut." Answers: "forgiven", "forgiveness", "I forgive". Hints from Stroud if they are stuck: "Silas Crane died hating everyone who owed him. What did he never do?" Wrong answers like "a grudge" or "a debt" are close; Stroud says "Yes, that is the thing. Now what do you do with it?"
- **Kettridge's Bargain.** Kettridge, sweating as midnight nears, invites the heroes to dinner in the Gilded Hen: steak, rye, candles. He offers anyone who signs the "receipt" 10,000 dollars, Abner's IOU, Mateo's father's land returned. If a hero lifts the receipt (Wits DC 13, or Stroud warns them), underneath is the keeper's line. If a hero signs, they become keeper and inherit "forty-one lives, due midnight."
- **Breaking Kettridge.** To close the book himself, Kettridge must mean forgiveness. He can be broken: confronting him with proof (Plum's page), with Mateo's father's death, and with Stroud's confession, makes him remember he once was a land agent who believed in the railroad. Spirit DC 19, with +2 for each of the three pieces of proof presented. If broken, he writes "Forgiven" and it holds.
- **Rosalind's Hour.** 10:00. Rosalind Achebe, a widowed rancher in the first passenger car, is due for her water rights. She can sign them over (a sad but safe choice), or the heroes can prevent her accident (a coal-oil lamp tips; Agility DC 13 to catch it, or move her to the dining car beforehand).

**Clues:**
- The keepership can be passed by signing: Stroud, or lifting the pasted receipt.
- The book closes with "Forgiven": solving the riddle, with Stroud's hints.
- A rescued debtor is free: seeing Plum or Hollis saved, or Stroud realizing it aloud.

**Ends when:** it is 11:30 and the train is half an hour from Coldwater Canyon.

### Act 4: Coldwater Trestle

**Goal:** close the Ledger or survive it, at the trestle at midnight.

**Scenes:**
- **The Charges.** At 11:00 Mercy's men set six sticks of dynamite under the trestle's middle span, with a long fuse to be lit when the engine's light appears. If the heroes know, Winnie can telegraph from the train's emergency key to Coldwater station to stop the train (it will not stop for Kettridge's orders, only the engineer's); or Smoke Iwu can stop the engine short of the bridge (Spirit DC 13 to convince him); or heroes ride ahead from the last stop on a borrowed horse.
- **The Trestle.** 400 feet long, 300 feet above a dry river, timber and iron, moonlight. If the train stops on or before the bridge, Kettridge, in a panic, runs out onto the trestle with the Ledger, Hobb behind him.
- **The Showdown.** Mercy and her riders on the canyon rim; Kettridge and Hobb on the bridge; the heroes between. Everyone wants the book: Mercy to burn the man, Stroud to save Kettridge's soul, Kettridge to make someone sign. Talking can turn every side. Hobb will lower his gun if shown Plum's page.
- **Midnight.** Every watch ticks together. Whoever is keeper and has not written "Forgiven" and meant it, the Ledger collects: the wind takes them off the trestle. If the keeper is a hero, they can still write it in the last seconds (Spirit DC 13 to mean it while terrified, with +2 if they forgive the debt owed them in their hook aloud: Jo forgives Mercy, Abner forgives himself, Mateo forgives the railroad, Winnie forgives herself for the telegram, Ruth forgives the gang).

**Ends when:** midnight has passed and the book is closed, kept, or lost in the canyon.

## NPCs

- **Ambrose Kettridge**, railroad baron, 60. *Voice:* warm, rich, generous-sounding, uses everyone's first name, snaps his watch shut at the end of a sentence. *Wants:* someone to sign before midnight. *Knows:* every name he has written. *Secret:* he is terrified. *If threatened:* offers money, then calls Hobb.
- **Reverend Elijah Stroud**, 70. *Voice:* thunderous sermon voice that cracks into a whisper when he talks about himself. *Wants:* to save Kettridge and his own soul. *Secret:* former keeper. *If threatened:* prays loudly.
- **Mercy Dorrance**, outlaw leader, 30. *Voice:* lazy drawl, calls everyone "sugar", goes deadly quiet before violence. *Wants:* Hollis free, Kettridge ruined. *If threatened:* shoots first.
- **Hollis Dorrance**, 22. *Voice:* sings hymns off-key, jokes about his own hanging. *Wants:* to live and keep the ranch.
- **Lucius Hobb**, Blackwell detective. *Voice:* flat, one-word answers, chews a match. *Wants:* his pay. *If shown proof:* walks away.
- **Wendell Plum**, conductor. *Voice:* nervous, announces everything ("Ash Creek, twenty minutes!"). *Secret:* the page in his cap.
- **Rosalind Achebe**, rancher. *Voice:* dry, says "I reckon not."
- **Bartholomew "Smoke" Iwu**, engineer. *Voice:* booming, calls the engine "the old girl".
- **Seth Dorrance**, 16, gang member. *Voice:* cracking, tries to sound tough.

## Locations

- **Bitterroot Flats.** Platform, water troughs, the Busted Spoke saloon.
- **The Sorrel Flyer.** Engine, tender, mail car with safe and Hollis, baggage car with crates of rifles, two passenger cars, dining car with white cloths, the Gilded Hen.
- **Sweetgrass.** A tiny stop, telegraph shack, view of the Salazar ranch.
- **Ash Creek.** Water tower, pump shack, cottonwoods, dry creek bed.
- **Coldwater Canyon trestle.** 400 feet long, 300 feet high, timber and iron, a wind that moans.
- **Paloma Junction.** End of the line: gallows, railroad office, Sheriff Cardoza.

## Threats

- **Dorrance Gang** (Mercy plus 4). HP 10 each, Mercy 14. Attack +3, revolver d6, Mercy +4 d8. Will retreat if two fall or if Hollis is returned.
- **Lucius Hobb.** HP 14. Attack +4, revolver d8. Surrenders when shown proof or outnumbered three to one.
- **The Ledger.** Cannot be harmed. Collects at the hour by accident. A debtor rescued from their accident is free.
- **Falling from the trestle or train.** d10 damage, or death from the canyon; a hero who falls into the canyon can be caught on a beam (Agility DC 13).

## Clocks

- **The Hours:** 1) 4:00 Hollis; 2) 7:00 Plum; 3) 10:00 Rosalind; 4) midnight Kettridge, or whoever has signed.
- **The Gang's Plan:** 1) 5:00 robbery at Ash Creek; 2) 8:00 they ride for the canyon; 3) 11:00 charges set; 4) midnight fuse lit when the engine's light appears.
- **Kettridge's Desperation:** 1) friendly offers; 2) writes a hero's name; 3) has Hobb hold a hero at gunpoint to sign; 4) runs onto the trestle with the book.

## Rewards

- Kettridge's money: 10,000 dollars, if someone signs (a trap).
- Plum's page: proof that ends the Sorrel and Pacific's land grabs.
- Jo's bounty: 500 dollars for Hollis delivered alive.
- Mercy's friendship: the gang owes any hero who saved Hollis one favor.
- Ash Creek ranch: Hollis offers half to whoever saved him.
- The closed Ledger: an ordinary empty book; every entry struck in red.

## Endings

- **Triumph (Paid in Full):** someone writes "Forgiven" and means it. Every line turns red. Kettridge lives, broken and humbled, and gives back land. Stroud weeps.
- **Bittersweet (The Keeper's Fall):** the Ledger collects Kettridge off the trestle at midnight, but the book falls with him into the canyon, unclosed, waiting for someone to find it.
- **Disaster (New Keeper):** a hero signs and does not close the book; the Ledger takes them at midnight, or they keep it, and become the next Kettridge.

## Off the Map

- **People:** Sheriff Ines Cardoza (Paloma), Deacon Moss Abernathy, Lottie Vance (singer), Jebediah Coil (prospector), Doc Hannah Weaver.
- **Places:** Sweetgrass Spring, Salazar ranch, Crane's grave in Bitterroot cemetery, the Paloma gallows.
- **If they leave the story:** if they leave the train, the hours still pass. Kettridge finds someone else to sign (Abner's IOU or a lonely porter), the Ledger rolls on west, and new names come due.
