# Salt and Sorrow

## Pitch

Forty years ago the treasure galleon Sorrow went down on the reef called the Teeth with thirty chests of silver and a pearl the size of a hen's egg. Now an old map has surfaced, your ship the Merrow's Hope has it, and so does the fastest crew in the Scattered Isles. Between you and the wreck lie three days of open water, a sea witch who wants payment, and a crew that is starting to whisper.

## DM Truths

1. The Sorrow was a merchant galleon carrying 30 chests of silver (each worth 100 gold) and the Widow's Pearl. Her captain, Bastian Crowe, stole the pearl from a sea cave: it is the hardened last tear of the sea witch Grandmother Skerry, shed when her drowned husband was not returned to her. She cursed the ship and it struck the Teeth in a calm sea.
2. One survivor: a fourteen-year-old cabin boy named Jory Marlowe, who clung to a hatch cover for two days. He is now Captain Jory Marlowe of the Merrow's Hope, sixty-one, with a bad cough and one last voyage in him. He drew the map from memory. He has never told anyone he was aboard.
3. The rival ship is the Red Gannet, a fast sloop under Captain Odile Vance. Odile is Jory Marlowe's daughter. He left her mother when Odile was six, to go to sea, and never came back. She learned to sail out of spite. She got a copy of the map because someone aboard the Merrow's Hope sold it to her.
4. The seller is the bosun, Silas Rook. Odile paid him 50 gold and promised 200 more and a berth as her mate if the Merrow's Hope fails to reach the wreck. Rook is stirring a mutiny: telling the crew the captain is dying and mad, that the witch will take their souls, and that the Red Gannet pays better.
5. The wreck lies in two halves on a sand shelf forty feet down inside the ring of the Teeth. Only one gap in the reef is safe for a ship: the Widow's Gate, on the north side, passable only at low tide. Grandmother Skerry knows this, and so does Jory, if he is pressed.
6. The drowned crew of the Sorrow, nineteen sailors, still haunt the wreck as the Drowned Sorrows. They cannot rest until their names are spoken aloud above the water. The names are in the Sorrow's logbook in the captain's cabin. Jory remembers seven of them.
7. Grandmother Skerry will give the heroes kelp-knots that let a person breathe underwater for one hour (enough for six), and tell them the way through the Widow's Gate. Her price, which she states plainly: the Widow's Pearl must come back to her. If they refuse, she will accept instead one hero's happiest memory, taken forever. Either price is real and she will collect.
8. If the Pearl is returned to her, the curse on the Teeth lifts, the Drowned Sorrows rest, and the sea there calms for good. If the Pearl is sold (a buyer in Port Vellum would pay 3,000 gold), Skerry's storms follow whoever holds it.

## Table Notes

Swashbuckling first, sorrow underneath. Keep the sea alive: weather, gulls, rope burns, rum. Act 1 is about 45 minutes in port; Act 2 an hour at sea with the witch; Act 3 an hour at the wreck; Act 4 the battle and reckoning, 45 to 60 minutes. The mutiny clock should tick between every scene at sea.

Content care: no torture; boarding fights are fast and cinematic, and defeated sailors surrender rather than die. Drowning is described as cold and dark, never prolonged. Jory's illness is a cough and pale hands, not graphic.

Recurring details: the creak of the Merrow's Hope sounds like an old woman humming; the smell of tar and salt pork; whenever Skerry is near, the sea turns glassy and gulls go silent. Jory's cough comes before every important thing he says.

## Characters

- **Nell Harrow**, first mate, scarred and loyal. Might +2, Agility +2, Wits +1, Spirit +0. HP 15. Gear: cutlass (d6), boarding axe (d8), spyglass. Hook: Jory pulled her out of a debtor's prison; she would die for him and suspects he is hiding something.
- **Tobias "Tick" Ferrow**, ship's carpenter and gossip. Might +3, Agility +0, Wits +1, Spirit +1. HP 16. Gear: heavy maul (d8), tool kit, tar bucket. Hook: he hears everything below decks and has noticed Rook buying drinks for the crew.
- **Esme Quarry**, navigator who reads stars and currents. Might +0, Agility +1, Wits +3, Spirit +1. HP 11. Gear: brace of pistols (d8, one shot each, reload takes a turn), sextant, charts. Hook: her mother drowned on the Teeth; she wants to see the reef that killed her.
- **Brother Kell**, ship's cook and former monk of the sea-god. Might +1, Agility +0, Wits +1, Spirit +3. HP 13. Gear: cleaver (d6), holy salt (heals d6, 3 uses), a battered prayer bell. Hook: his order says the dead of the sea speak to those who listen; he can hear the Sorrows faintly from the start.
- **Wick Daley**, ship's boy, quick and cheeky, fourteen. Might +0, Agility +3, Wits +2, Spirit +0. HP 10. Gear: knife (d6), slingshot (d6), monkey named Biscuit. Hook: Jory treats him like a son, and Wick does not know why.
- **Rosalind Thane**, disgraced naval officer turned privateer. Might +1, Agility +2, Wits +2, Spirit +0. HP 13. Gear: rapier (d6), naval pistol (d8, one shot), a stolen commission. Hook: she knew Odile Vance at the naval academy and once loved her.

## Opening Scene

Night in the harbor of Port Vellum. Lanterns sway over the Gallows Wharf; a fiddle saws in the Crooked Anchor tavern. The heroes stand on the deck of the Merrow's Hope, a two-masted brigantine with a crew of eighteen. Captain Jory Marlowe coughs into a handkerchief, unrolls a map on a barrel, and says: "Three days to the Teeth. The Sorrow's down there, and she's ours." Then Wick runs up the gangplank, out of breath: the Red Gannet just slipped her moorings, and Wick heard her captain say "the Teeth." First choice: sail now, half-provisioned, or spend the night in port finding out how the Gannet knew.

## Spoken Opening

Night in the harbor of Port Vellum. Lanterns sway above the Gallows Wharf, and from the Crooked Anchor tavern a fiddle saws away at a reel. The air is thick with tar and salt pork.

You stand on the deck of the Merrow's Hope, a two-masted brigantine with a crew of eighteen. Her old timbers creak beneath you, and the sound is almost like an old woman humming.

Captain Jory Marlowe, gray and thin, coughs into a handkerchief. Then he unrolls a weathered map across a barrel head and pins its corners with his pale hands. Three days to the Teeth, he tells you. The Sorrow lies down there, forty years drowned, with her silver and her famous pearl. And she is yours.

Before anyone can cheer, a deckhand comes pounding up the gangplank, out of breath. The Red Gannet, the fastest crew in the Scattered Isles, has just slipped her moorings. Her captain was heard to name one place as she cast off. The Teeth.

The ship is half provisioned. The tide is turning. Do you sail tonight, or stay and learn how the Gannet knew?

## Acts

### Act 1: Port Vellum

**Goal:** Prepare to sail, and learn how the secret got out.

**Scenes:**
- **The Crooked Anchor tavern.** Hawker Pim, a one-eyed barman, sold drinks to Silas Rook two nights ago while Rook talked with a woman in a red coat. Paying Pim 2 gold or a Spirit roll DC 13 gets this. Pim adds: the woman paid Rook "in a fat purse."
- **The Chandlery.** Mistress Grell sells supplies: powder, shot, food. A full load costs 30 gold; the ship has 40. Skipping this means only 3 days of food and 10 shots of cannon powder instead of 20.
- **Rook's hammock.** Searching it (Agility DC 13 to do so unseen) finds a purse with 50 gold stamped with a red gannet, and a half-burned note: "200 more when the Hope fails. O.V."
- **Jory's cabin.** Jory will say only that he "knows those waters." Wits DC 16, or Nell pressing him as an old friend, gets more: "I've been on the Teeth before. Leave it there."

**Clues:** Rook sold the map (Pim's story; or the purse; or Tick's gossip). Odile Vance commands the Gannet (Pim; or Rosalind recognizes the red coat). Jory has a past with the Sorrow (his cabin; or Brother Kell hears the drowned whisper "Jory" when they near the reef).

**Ends when:** The Merrow's Hope sets sail.

### Act 2: The Open Sea and Gull Rock

**Goal:** Gain on the Gannet, win Skerry's help, and keep the crew together.

**Scenes:**
- **First day at sea.** The Gannet is visible as a speck, two leagues ahead. Esme can plot a shortcut through the Mackerel Shoals (Wits DC 13): success gains half a day; failure scrapes the hull and Tick must repair it (Might DC 10).
- **Rook's whispers.** At the evening meal, a sailor named Dunny Coyle spits on the deck and says the captain is "dying and mad." The crew of eighteen splits: 6 loyal, 6 wavering, 6 with Rook. Speeches (Spirit DC 13), extra rum (a barrel), or exposing Rook with the purse each win over the wavering six.
- **Gull Rock.** A pillar of black rock where a thousand gulls nest; at its foot, a sea cave. The sea goes glassy, the gulls go silent. Grandmother Skerry sits in the cave on a throne of driftwood: a tiny, ancient woman with seaweed hair and eyes like wet pebbles. She offers kelp-knots for six divers, and the safe way through the Teeth: the Widow's Gate, at low tide, which comes at dawn on the third day. Her price: the Widow's Pearl, returned to her hand. Or, if they will not promise that, one hero's happiest memory, taken now. She tells them plainly what the Pearl is: "My last tear. I wept it the night the sea would not give my Aldous back."
- **The Gannet's ambush (optional).** If the heroes dawdled, the Red Gannet doubles back at night and fires two cannon shots across the bow as a warning. Odile hails them by speaking-trumpet: "Go home, old man!" Jory goes white.

**Clues:** The Pearl is Skerry's tear (Skerry tells them; or Brother Kell's order tells of a witch who wept a pearl). The Widow's Gate is the only safe way in (Skerry; or Jory, if pressed with Spirit DC 16; or Esme reading wreckage patterns at the reef with Wits DC 16). Odile is Jory's daughter (Rosalind knows Odile's mother's name was Marlowe; or Jory confesses after the ambush).

**Ends when:** The Merrow's Hope reaches the Teeth.

### Act 3: The Teeth and the Wreck

**Goal:** Get through the reef and recover the treasure.

**Scenes:**
- **The Widow's Gate.** At dawn low tide, a gap of forty feet between two coral horns. Esme or Jory steers (Wits DC 13). Failure: the hull scrapes, 1 step on the Hull clock. Without Skerry's directions, the only other ways in are rowboats, which hold four each.
- **The Red Gannet.** Already anchored inside the ring, having lost a boat on the coral. Odile's crew of twenty is lowering a diving bell. She has three divers in iron helmets.
- **The wreck.** Forty feet down, on white sand, in two halves.
 - The bow half: the hold, with 30 iron-banded chests of silver, each needing two people to lift (Might DC 10 with a line to the surface). Bringing up each chest takes ten minutes. A giant moray named Old Gnash lives in the anchor-hold.
 - The stern half: the captain's cabin, door jammed (Might DC 13). Inside: a skeleton in a captain's coat, the logbook in a waxed leather case, and a small iron box with the Widow's Pearl. It is warm to the touch and makes the water around it taste of tears.
- **The Drowned Sorrows.** When anyone enters the stern, nineteen pale shapes rise from the sand, sailors in rotted clothes with seaweed for hair. They do not attack at first. They drift close and say, all together: "Say our names." If anyone tries to take the Pearl before the names are spoken, they attack (see Threats). The names are in the logbook's crew list. Jory remembers seven: Hallam, Bess Corrigan, Little Tam, Old Saul, Mercy Hale, the Dutchman Pieter, and Captain Crowe. Speaking all nineteen names above the water at the ship's rail puts them to rest, and they will guard the heroes' divers against the Gannet's.
- **The logbook names.** The remaining twelve: Absalom Fry, Nan Whistler, Gil Harte, Ruth Penhallow, Cobb, Jonah Stray, Matthias Lark, Ebba Lund, Rafe Tolliver, Hester Quill, Ned Barrow, and the cook, Mother Abigail.

**Clues:** The names are needed (the Sorrows say it; or Brother Kell hears it). The logbook has the names (it is in the cabin; or Jory knows to look). Jory was on the Sorrow (the logbook lists "J. Marlowe, cabin boy, age 14").

**Ends when:** The treasure is up, or the Gannet strikes.

### Act 4: Broadsides and Reckoning

**Goal:** Survive the Gannet's attack and Rook's mutiny, and decide the Pearl's fate.

**Scenes:**
- **Rook makes his move.** When the Gannet attacks, Rook and his loyal sailors try to seize the wheel and strike the colors. How many he has depends on the Mutiny clock. Exposing his note in front of the crew (Spirit DC 10) breaks him on the spot.
- **Broadsides.** Each ship has cannons (Gannet 8, Hope 10). Each round, the heroes choose: fire (gunnery Wits DC 13, hit = Gannet Hull clock +1), maneuver (Esme or Jory, Wits DC 13, avoid Gannet's fire), or board. The Gannet fires every round: Agility DC 13 by the helmsman or the Hope's Hull clock +1.
- **Boarding.** Swinging across on lines, Agility DC 10. Odile fights on her quarterdeck. She surrenders if her crew breaks or Jory faces her.
- **Father and daughter.** If Jory and Odile meet face to face, he coughs and says, "You have your mother's eyes, Odette." Only he calls her that. What follows is up to the heroes: they can broker peace, share the treasure, or let her go.
- **The Pearl.** Returning it to Skerry at Gull Rock (or dropping it overboard while saying her name, which she accepts) calms the sea. Selling it means a storm follows the ship for a year and a day.

**Ends when:** One ship sails away from the Teeth.

## NPCs

- **Captain Jory Marlowe**, captain of the Merrow's Hope. *Voice:* rasping, with a cough before anything important; calls the heroes "lads and lasses" regardless. *Wants:* to see the Sorrow once more and make it right. *Knows:* the reef, seven names, the Widow's Gate. *Secret:* he was the cabin boy; Odile is his daughter. *If threatened:* stands still and dares them.
- **Captain Odile Vance**, captain of the Red Gannet. *Voice:* clipped, sharp, nautical orders; never says please. *Wants:* to beat her father at the one thing he loved more than her. *Knows:* the map from Rook. *Secret:* she still has the wooden gull he carved for her. *If threatened:* fights hard, surrenders to save her crew.
- **Silas Rook**, bosun. *Voice:* low and friendly, every sentence ending in "eh, mate?" *Wants:* a ship of his own. *Knows:* Odile's offer. *If threatened:* blames Dunny Coyle, then begs.
- **Grandmother Skerry**, sea witch. *Voice:* creaky and slow, speaks of herself as "the old sea"; laughs like water draining from a rock pool. *Wants:* her tear back. *Knows:* everything about the Teeth. *If threatened:* the sea rises; a wave knocks everyone flat. She cannot be fought.
- **Hawker Pim**, barman. *Voice:* wheezy and gossipy, winks after every secret. *Knows:* Rook met "a woman in red."
- **Mistress Grell**, chandler. *Voice:* brisk, counts money aloud as she talks.
- **Dunny Coyle**, sailor and Rook's mouthpiece. *Voice:* loud and slurring. *Wants:* rum and fair pay. Switches sides if given either.
- **Old Gnash**, a giant moray. No voice; a hiss and a click of teeth.

## Locations

- **Port Vellum.** Gallows Wharf, the Crooked Anchor tavern, Grell's Chandlery. Fish guts, fiddles, gull cries.
- **The Merrow's Hope.** A two-masted brigantine, 10 cannons, crew of 18, a figurehead of a mermaid with a chipped nose.
- **The Red Gannet.** A fast sloop, 8 cannons, crew of 20, a diving bell, red sails.
- **The Mackerel Shoals.** Shallow water, flashing fish, a shortcut.
- **Gull Rock.** Black stone, a thousand silent gulls, Skerry's cave with a driftwood throne.
- **The Teeth.** A ring of coral reef; inside it a calm green lagoon. The Widow's Gate on the north side.
- **The wreck of the Sorrow.** Two halves on white sand, forty feet down; hold with 30 chests; stern cabin with the logbook and Pearl.

## Threats

- **Red Gannet sailors** (20). HP 6 each. Attack +3, d6 cutlass. Surrender when: Odile surrenders or half are down.
- **Captain Odile Vance.** HP 15. Attack +5, rapier d6, pistol d8. Special: twice per fight she can disarm a foe (Agility DC 13 to keep hold).
- **Silas Rook.** HP 12. Attack +4, belaying pin d6.
- **Mutineers** (up to 6). HP 6 each. Attack +2, d6. Surrender when: Rook is down or exposed.
- **Old Gnash, the moray.** HP 18. Attack +5, d8 bite, holds on (Might DC 13 to break free). Leaves if fed a salt pork side.
- **The Drowned Sorrows** (19, fight as one swarm). HP 20 as a swarm. Attack +4, d6 cold. Special: they rest if their names are spoken. Calm if Brother Kell rings his prayer bell.
- **The Hull clocks.** Each ship has a 4-step Hull clock. At 4, the ship is crippled and must strike its colors.

## Clocks

- **The Race:** 1) The Gannet reaches the Teeth. 2) The Gannet gets its bell down. 3) Gannet divers raise 10 chests. 4) Gannet divers break into the stern cabin and take the Pearl. Advances one step each time the heroes linger.
- **Mutiny:** 1) Rook wins over the wavering six. 2) Rook's men refuse an order. 3) The loyal six are locked below. 4) Rook seizes the wheel. Ticks after each scene at sea unless the heroes act.
- **Storm (if the Pearl is sold or Skerry is cheated):** 1) Gulls follow the ship. 2) Rain. 3) A gale splits a mast. 4) The sea takes the ship.

## Rewards

- The silver: 30 chests at 100 gold each; however much the heroes raise is theirs.
- Rook's purse: 50 gold.
- The Widow's Pearl: 3,000 gold if sold, with the Storm clock attached.
- Skerry's blessing if the Pearl is returned: the Merrow's Hope never sinks while her crew is honest, and one favor from the sea witch.
- The Drowned Sorrows' gift if laid to rest: Captain Crowe's compass, which always points to safe water.
- The Red Gannet, if taken.

## Endings

- **Triumph:** The Pearl goes home to Skerry, the Sorrows rest, Rook is exposed, and Jory and Odile meet as father and daughter. The two ships sail home together. Jory dies in his bunk months later, having spoken with his daughter every day of the voyage.
- **Bittersweet:** The treasure is won but the Pearl is sold, or one hero loses their happiest memory. Jory and Odile do not reconcile; he dies without telling her the truth, and she reads it in the logbook years later.
- **Disaster:** The mutiny succeeds or the Gannet takes the Pearl. Skerry's storm finds the ship, and the heroes wash up on a rocky island with what they can carry.
