## Pitch

Two or three sentences Delamain reads to the car to open the game. Sets the hook and the mood; no secrets.

## DM Truths

What is really going on, from the start of the story to now. The secrets, who did what and why, the twist. Delamain never says these outright; the players uncover them through play. Number them so they are easy to keep straight.

1. ...
2. ...

## Table Notes

How the game should feel, the pace across a long drive, content to handle carefully (and what to fade out), and two or three sensory details to come back to so the world feels consistent (a smell, a sound, a recurring image).

## Characters

Four to six ready-made heroes, for players who would rather not make their own. Each one line of concept, then the numbers, then why they are here.

- **Name**, short concept. Might +2, Agility +1, Wits +0, Spirit +1. HP 14. Gear: ... Hook: why this hero is in the story, and one personal stake.

## Opening Scene

Where the heroes are when the game begins, what they see, hear and smell, and the first choice in front of them. Delamain narrates this first, then asks each player who they are.

## Acts

Three to five acts, roughly 45 to 75 minutes each. For every act: its goal, its scenes, and how it ends.

### Act 1: Title

**Goal:** what the heroes are trying to do in this act.

**Scenes:**
- **Scene name.** What is here. What the heroes can learn (exact facts), and the roll it takes, if any. What goes wrong. Where it leads.

**Clues:** the facts this act must be able to give out, each with at least two ways to find it, so the story cannot get stuck behind one missed roll.

**Ends when:** ...

## NPCs

Everyone with a name. Since one voice plays them all, give each a way to sound different in narration ("she speaks in clipped, nautical orders"; "he laughs at his own jokes before finishing them").

- **Name**, role. *Voice:* how Delamain portrays them. *Wants:* ... *Knows:* ... *Secret:* ... *If threatened:* ...

## Locations

Every place the story can go, with two or three concrete details each and what can be found there.

- **Place.** ...

## Threats

Foes and dangers with simple numbers, and the ways around a fight (talking, sneaking, bargaining, running).

- **Name** (how many). HP 8 each. Attack +3, d6 damage. Special: ... Wants: ... Will flee or surrender when: ...

## Clocks

What happens if the heroes dawdle, go elsewhere, or make noise: the world moves without them. Three or four steps each.

- **Clock name:** 1) ... 2) ... 3) ... 4) ...

## Rewards

Treasure, allies, secrets and favors that can be won, with exact amounts and effects.

## Endings

At least three endings: a triumph, a bittersweet one and a disaster, and the choices that lead to each. Delamain closes the game with the one the heroes earned, in a short epilogue for each hero.

## Off the Map

Players will go where nobody planned. Name lists for improvised people, places and taverns that fit the setting (Delamain takes from here instead of inventing, so names stay in the world), and what the world does if the heroes ignore the plot entirely.

- **People:** ...
- **Places:** ...
- **If they leave the story:** ...
