# The Lantern at Gull's Rest

## Pitch

Gull's Rest is a fishing village of forty-one houses clinging to the cliffs of the Morrow Coast. Six nights ago its lighthouse keeper, Osric Venn, vanished, and since then the lamp on Harrow Point has lit itself every dusk and burned green. Two boats have followed that light onto the rocks. The village wants answers before tonight's tide, and you have just arrived on the morning cart.

## DM Truths

1. Forty-one years ago, on the night of the autumn storm, the village elders lit a false light on Harrow Point and lured the merchant ship *Calliope Grey* onto the reef called the Teeth. They looted her cargo of silver and wool. All 23 crew drowned. The lighthouse keeper then, Aldous Venn (Osric's father), lit the false light himself.
2. Of the seven wreckers, three are still alive: Hester Crane (78, mother of the innkeeper), Pell Danby (70, the harbormaster), and Aldous's old partner Gideon Shaw (74, the chandler). The other four are buried in the village churchyard.
3. The *Calliope Grey*'s captain was Ysolde Marrow. Her fury did not drown with her. She and her crew sleep under the Teeth as the Drowned, bound to the reef.
4. Two weeks ago Osric found his dead father's confession, a black oilskin ledger hidden behind a loose brick in the lamp room, listing the wreckers and the 23 crew names. Sick with guilt, Osric rowed to the Teeth at the new moon and dropped a page into the sea, saying aloud, "I will light your way home."
5. Captain Ysolde took him at his word. She took the lamp's flame and made it green corpse-light, which guides the Drowned ashore. She took Osric too: he is alive, half-drowned and entranced, chained by kelp in the sea cave under the lighthouse called the Gullet. He tends the green flame by night through the old smugglers' stair.
6. The torn ledger is in Osric's cottage, hidden inside the mattress. The page Osric threw into the sea was the page of crew names. A second copy of the 23 names exists: the ship's manifest in the Salt Office records at the Customs House.
7. Each night the Drowned come closer. Tonight, the seventh night, at the midnight high tide, they will walk up the Gullet stair and into the village to take 23 lives in payment, starting with the three living wreckers.
8. The Drowned can be laid to rest three ways: the truth spoken aloud at the Teeth by a living wrecker, all 23 crew names read aloud at the lamp, and the green flame replaced by a true flame. Any two of the three is enough to end the haunting. The flame alone only delays it one night.
9. A true flame is fire carried from a hearth that has never gone out. The only one in the village is the hearth at the Drowned Gull inn, kept burning for 112 years.
10. The silver from the *Calliope Grey* (about 300 silver marks) built the village's chapel roof and the harbor wall. Pell Danby still keeps 40 marks of it in a strongbox under his floor.

## Table Notes

Slow dread, not gore. Start quiet and ordinary: gulls, nets, gossip. Let the wrongness rise as the green grows brighter and the fog thickens. The Drowned are terrifying at a distance; up close they are sad, cold, and dripping. Fade out any drowning description after a sentence. Deaths of NPCs happen off-scene and are found, not shown.

Pace: Act 1 is the village by daylight, Act 2 is the afternoon digging into the past, Act 3 is dusk at the lighthouse, Act 4 is the midnight tide. Remind players of the time of day as acts change.

Recurring details: the smell of tar and wet wool; a bell buoy that clangs twice whenever the Drowned are near even when the sea is flat; the word "home" whispered in the sea foam. Green light shows on faces, in puddles, in eyes.

## Characters

- **Wren Halloway**, Osric's niece, a sharp-eyed net mender. Might +0, Agility +2, Wits +2, Spirit +1. HP 12. Gear: gutting knife (d6), whistle, her uncle's spare key to the lighthouse. Hook: Osric raised her after her parents died; she was the last to see him, and he said, "If I am late, keep the hearth lit."
- **Brother Tamsin Ash**, a wandering priest of the Lamp, the faith of lighthouse and hearth. Might +1, Agility +0, Wits +1, Spirit +3. HP 13. Gear: blessed lantern, staff (d6), salt and candles. Hook: the green light is a blasphemy against his order; his own sister drowned at sea and was never buried.
- **Corin Duskwater**, an inspector of the Salt Office, the coastal customs authority. Might +0, Agility +1, Wits +3, Spirit +1. HP 12. Gear: rapier (d6), warrant seal, notebook, lockpicks. Hook: sent because two wrecks were reported; he has the authority to open the Customs House records and arrest anyone.
- **Maeve Rook**, a fisherwoman with a harpoon and a temper. Might +3, Agility +1, Wits +0, Spirit +0. HP 16. Gear: harpoon (d8), boat called the *Sorrel*, oilskin coat. Hook: her younger brother Tobin followed the green light four nights ago and has not come home. He is alive, washed into the Gullet cave.
- **Jory Fitch**, a charming smuggler and locksmith. Might +0, Agility +3, Wits +1, Spirit +0. HP 11. Gear: two knives (d6), rope, lockpicks, a flask of strong rum. Hook: he owes Osric 20 silver marks and used the smugglers' stair under the lighthouse years ago; he knows it exists.

## Opening Scene

Morning. The supply cart creaks down the cliff road into Gull's Rest under a sky the color of old pewter. The village smells of tar, fish and peat smoke. Gulls wheel and scream. Out on Harrow Point, a mile north, the white lighthouse stands dark in daylight, but a fishwife pauses to say, without being asked, "It'll be green again tonight. Green as a drowned man's eye." The cart stops at the Drowned Gull inn, where innkeeper Nell Crane waits on the step with her arms folded. Down at the harbor, a crowd gathers around something wrapped in sailcloth.

First choice: the inn, the harbor crowd, or straight to the lighthouse. Ask each player who they are and why they came.

## Spoken Opening

Morning, under a sky the color of old pewter. The supply cart creaks down the cliff road into Gull's Rest, forty-one houses clinging to the rock above a grey and restless sea. The village smells of tar, fish, and peat smoke. Gulls wheel overhead, screaming.

A mile to the north, out on Harrow Point, the white lighthouse stands dark against the sky. Six nights ago its keeper, Osric Venn, vanished. Every dusk since, his lamp has lit itself, and burned green. Two boats have followed that light onto the rocks.

As the cart rolls past, a fishwife looks up from her nets. Nobody asked her, but she tells you anyway. It will be green again tonight, she says. Green as a drowned man's eye.

The cart stops before the Drowned Gull inn. On the step, the innkeeper, Nell Crane, waits with her arms folded, watching you climb down. And below, at the harbor, a crowd is gathering around something wrapped in sailcloth.

The tide will turn tonight, and the village wants answers before it does. Where do you go first, the inn, the harbor, or the lighthouse?

## Acts

### Act 1: The Village by Daylight

**Goal:** learn what happened to Osric and why the lamp burns green.

**Scenes:**
- **The Harbor Crowd.** The sailcloth covers a broken oar marked *Tobin R*, washed in at dawn. Harbormaster Pell Danby orders the crowd to disperse, sweating. A child, Pip Moss, says she saw "wet people standing in the surf" last night and that they "were counting." Wits DC 13 notices Pell glancing up at the chapel roof and crossing himself.
- **The Drowned Gull Inn.** Nell Crane serves fish stew for 1 copper. She says Osric came in eight nights ago, drank alone, and asked her mother Hester "how many lived through the Calliope." Hester, by the fire, went white and left the room. Nell will mention the hearth: "Never gone out in a hundred and twelve years. My great-grandfather swore it." Spirit DC 13 to calm Hester; she only says, "Ask Aldous. Oh, he's dead. Then ask the sea."
- **Osric's Cottage.** A stone cottage at the cliff base, door unlocked. The bed is unmade, a bowl of cold porridge on the table, boots gone. Wits DC 13 (or anyone who sits on the bed) finds the oilskin ledger in the mattress. It is the confession of Aldous Venn and names the seven wreckers. One page is torn out. A scrawl in Osric's hand on the last page: "I will light your way home. God forgive us."
- **The Lighthouse by Day.** Locked; Wren has a key, or Agility DC 13 to pick. Inside: 112 spiral steps, a kitchen with a cold stove, the keeper's log. The last entry, seven days ago: "Rowed to the Teeth. Gave them the names. The sea said thank you." The lamp room smells of brine; the lamp wick is dry and cold to the touch, and the glass is crusted with salt on the inside. The loose brick behind the lamp is empty.

**Clues:**
- A shipwreck called the *Calliope Grey* happened long ago: Hester's reaction at the inn, or the confession ledger.
- The wreck was deliberate and the village profited: the ledger, or Pell's guilt (Spirit DC 13 to make him talk).
- Osric went to the Teeth: the keeper's log, or Wren's memory of him asking to borrow Maeve's boat.

**Ends when:** the heroes know about the *Calliope Grey* and the ledger, or it reaches midday.

### Act 2: What the Sea Remembers

**Goal:** uncover the full truth and find a way to lay the Drowned to rest.

**Scenes:**
- **The Customs House.** A shuttered office on the harbor. Corin's warrant opens it; otherwise Agility DC 13 or bribe the clerk Amos Pike 5 silver. The ship's manifest of the *Calliope Grey* lists all 23 crew by name, from Captain Ysolde Marrow down to cabin boy Wil Penhallow, aged 12. Copying or taking the manifest is essential for the reading of names.
- **The Churchyard.** Four wreckers' graves, all with fresh seaweed laid across them every morning. A sexton, old Bartholomew Lusk, says the seaweed began six days ago. A fifth grave is Aldous Venn's, and his headstone has cracked straight across.
- **The Chandler's Shop.** Gideon Shaw, 74, has packed a bag to flee inland. If confronted, Wits DC 13 or Spirit DC 13 makes him confess everything in DM Truth 1. He knows the old wreckers' rhyme: "Flame from the hearth that never sleeps, and names to the sea that the sea still keeps, and truth from the lips that lit the lie, and the drowned will rest and the light will die." He will not go near the sea.
- **Pell Danby's House.** Pell keeps 40 silver marks under the floor. He denies everything until shown the ledger, then begs the heroes to hide him. He is the most likely to run.
- **Brother Tamsin's Lore (or Wits DC 16 for anyone).** Corpse-light guides the dead, not the living. The dead come for what is owed. They can be turned by truth, by naming, and by true flame.
- **Optional: Rowing to the Teeth by daylight.** Maeve's *Sorrel* can get there in 20 minutes. Agility DC 13 to steer through the rocks; failure means d6 damage to someone and the boat takes a gash. At the reef, the bell buoy clangs twice. A voice under the water says, "Who keeps the light?" If the heroes answer "the living," the voice says, "Then bring us home before the seventh tide." If they speak any crew name, the water stills and a drowned hand gently returns Tobin's knife.

**Clues:**
- The rhyme (three ways to lay them to rest): Gideon Shaw's confession, or Brother Tamsin's lore, or a Wits DC 16 search of the chapel records.
- The 23 names: the manifest at the Customs House, or Captain Ysolde herself in Act 4 who will recite them if asked politely.
- The true flame is the inn's hearth: Nell Crane says so in Act 1, or Brother Tamsin knows "a hearth never quenched."

**Ends when:** the heroes have a plan, or the sun sets.

### Act 3: Dusk on Harrow Point

**Goal:** reach the lamp, face what tends it, and find Osric.

**Scenes:**
- **The Green Lighting.** At sunset the lamp lights itself. Every puddle in the village shows green. Fog rolls in thick enough that you cannot see your hand at arm's length.
- **The Climb.** 112 steps. Halfway, wet footprints come down toward the heroes, though nobody passes. Each hero rolls Spirit DC 13 or loses 2 HP to a grave-deep cold.
- **The Lamp Room.** Osric Venn stands at the lamp, eyes milky, coat soaked, feeding the flame with kelp. He does not answer to his name except from Wren (who can wake him with Spirit DC 10) or anyone holding the ledger (Spirit DC 13). If attacked, he fights weakly. Two Drowned sailors rise from the stairwell as guards.
- **The Smugglers' Stair.** A trapdoor under the lamp-room rug (Jory knows; others Wits DC 13) leads down 200 wet steps cut in rock into the Gullet. Tobin Rook is there, alive, wedged on a ledge, shivering. Freeing him from kelp takes Might DC 13.
- **Quenching the Green.** The green flame can be smothered with a wet coat but relights in one minute. Only a true flame from the inn's hearth, carried in a lantern without going out, replaces it. Carrying it up the cliff road in fog: Agility DC 13 or Spirit DC 13 to keep it lit; a failure means a return trip to the inn (15 minutes).

**Clues:**
- Osric, once woken, tells them: "She said she'd come home tonight. All of them. Midnight. I told her I'd light the way." He knows Ysolde's name.
- The Drowned guards whisper names; anyone listening (Wits DC 10) hears "Wil, Wil" — the cabin boy.

**Ends when:** Osric is freed or the midnight tide begins.

### Act 4: The Seventh Tide

**Goal:** lay the Drowned to rest before they take 23 lives.

**Scenes:**
- **The Rising.** At midnight the bell buoy clangs without stopping. The Drowned walk out of the sea at the Gullet and up the stair: 23 of them, led by Captain Ysolde Marrow, pale and crowned with weed.
- **The Parley.** Ysolde stops if spoken to by name. Her voice is like tide in a cave. She wants "the ones who lit the lie" and "a road home." She will accept the reading of the names and the truth from a wrecker's lips. She will not accept silver; offered it, she grows angry.
- **The Truth Spoken.** A living wrecker must say aloud at the water's edge what they did. Hester will if Nell asks her (or Spirit DC 16). Gideon will if he is safe and guarded. Pell will only if threatened or shown his family's danger.
- **The Reading of Names.** All 23 names read aloud at the lamp or the water's edge. Each name read stills one of the Drowned. Reading takes about two minutes.
- **The True Flame.** Lighting the lamp with the hearth fire turns it gold. The Drowned turn toward it like sleepers and walk back into the sea.
- **If it fails.** The Drowned march on the village. They take Pell first. The heroes can hold the inn door with fire; the Drowned will not cross a threshold with a lit hearth.

**Ends when:** two of the three rites are done, or dawn breaks.

## NPCs

- **Osric Venn**, the missing keeper, 52. *Voice:* soft, slow, guilty, every sentence trailing off into the sea. *Wants:* to atone. *Knows:* everything in the ledger, Ysolde's name. *Secret:* he hoped the Drowned would take him and spare the village. *If threatened:* stands still and lets it happen.
- **Captain Ysolde Marrow**, drowned captain of the *Calliope Grey*. *Voice:* low and echoing, every word pulled from deep water, never uses contractions. *Wants:* her crew taken home and the truth told. *Knows:* the names of her crew and who lit the false light. *Secret:* she is tired, more tired than angry. *If threatened:* she sinks into the water and her crew rises.
- **Nell Crane**, innkeeper of the Drowned Gull, 45. *Voice:* brisk, practical, calls everyone "love." *Wants:* her mother safe, her inn standing. *Knows:* the hearth story, that Osric was asking about the *Calliope*. *Secret:* she found a silver coin with a ship's crest under her mother's bed years ago. *If threatened:* reaches for the poker.
- **Hester Crane**, Nell's mother, 78, a wrecker. *Voice:* thin, querulous, repeats the last thing said to her. *Wants:* to die before anyone learns. *Knows:* everything. *Secret:* she was the one who carried the lamp oil up the hill. *If threatened:* weeps and confesses.
- **Pell Danby**, harbormaster, 70, a wrecker. *Voice:* blustering, loud, sweating, says "now then" before every sentence. *Wants:* to escape. *Knows:* where the silver went. *Secret:* 40 silver marks under his floorboards. *If threatened:* tries to run north on the cliff road.
- **Gideon Shaw**, chandler, 74, a wrecker. *Voice:* precise, whispery, a man who counts everything. *Wants:* to flee inland. *Knows:* the wreckers' rhyme. *If threatened:* confesses immediately.
- **Amos Pike**, Customs House clerk. *Voice:* nasal, fussy, quotes regulations. *Wants:* no trouble. *Knows:* where the manifest is filed. *If threatened:* hands over the key.
- **Tobin Rook**, Maeve's brother, 19. *Voice:* hoarse, teeth chattering. *Knows:* he followed the light and heard the Drowned singing names.
- **Pip Moss**, child, 9. *Voice:* bright, fearless. *Knows:* she saw the Drowned counting.
- **Bartholomew Lusk**, sexton. *Voice:* gravelly, slow, gallows humor. *Knows:* about the seaweed on the graves and Aldous's cracked headstone.

## Locations

- **The Drowned Gull Inn.** Low beams, a hearth that has burned 112 years, fish stew, a painted sign of a gull with closed eyes. Rooms 3 silver a night.
- **Osric's Cottage.** Stone, at the cliff base. The ledger is in the mattress.
- **The Lighthouse on Harrow Point.** 112 steps, a kitchen, the lamp room. A trapdoor under the rug leads to the smugglers' stair.
- **The Gullet.** A sea cave under the lighthouse, 200 steps down, tide-flooded at midnight, echoing.
- **The Teeth.** A reef a mile offshore, black rocks like jaws, the bell buoy.
- **The Customs House.** Shuttered office. The *Calliope Grey* manifest.
- **The Churchyard.** Four wreckers' graves and Aldous Venn's, all with fresh seaweed.
- **The Chapel.** Its fine slate roof was paid for with stolen silver; its records mention the wreck as "an act of God."

## Threats

- **Drowned Sailors** (2 in Act 3, up to 23 in Act 4). HP 8 each. Attack +3, d6 cold damage. Special: they cannot cross a threshold with a lit hearth; calling one by name makes it stop for one round. Wants: to go home. Will stop when: their name is read or the true flame burns.
- **Osric, entranced.** HP 10. Attack +1, d6. Wakes as described.
- **Captain Ysolde Marrow.** HP 20. Attack +5, d8 cold damage. Special: sinks into water and heals 5 HP per round. Will stop when: truth spoken and names read.
- **The Fog.** Spirit DC 13 to keep your bearings; failure means you wander for 15 minutes.
- **The Teeth.** Agility DC 13 to steer through; failure deals d6 to someone aboard.

## Clocks

- **The Seventh Tide:** 1) Morning: seaweed on graves. 2) Noon: Pell tries to flee; if not stopped, he is found drowned in a puddle by sunset. 3) Sunset: the green light ignites and fog rolls in. 4) Midnight: the Drowned rise.
- **Tobin's Breath:** 1) Afternoon: he is cold. 2) Sunset: hypothermic, needs warmth soon. 3) Midnight: the Gullet floods; he drowns unless rescued.
- **The Hearth:** if the heroes reveal their plan loudly, at dusk a wave smashes into the inn and the hearth gutters; it must be relit (Spirit DC 13) or the true flame is lost.

## Rewards

- 40 silver marks from Pell Danby, if they claim it (Ysolde will be angry if they keep it).
- Osric's gratitude: the lighthouse is theirs to use, and a spare boat.
- Nell Crane's free room and board for life.
- Corin's report earns a 50-silver-mark Salt Office reward.
- Brother Tamsin's order rewards him with a new title: Keeper of the Morrow Coast Light.
- Ysolde's gift if laid to rest: a sealed bottle holding the captain's compass, which always points toward home.

## Endings

- **Triumph:** two or three rites done before midnight, Osric and Tobin saved. The lamp burns gold. The Drowned sink singing. The wreckers face justice.
- **Bittersweet:** the rites done after the Drowned take Pell Danby, or Osric chooses to go with them into the sea. The light turns gold, but the village is quieter.
- **Disaster:** no rites by dawn. The Drowned take 23 lives, and Gull's Rest becomes a ghost village with a green light burning above it forever.
