# The Long Night Shift

## Pitch

The Night Heron Diner sits alone at Harlow Crossing on Highway 51, open twenty-four hours, neon sign buzzing in the rain. Tonight, November sixth, you each stopped in for coffee. At fourteen minutes past three in the morning, something terrible happens, and then you are back in your seats at half past eleven, the same song on the jukebox, the same rain on the glass. The night will not end until you work out why.

## DM Truths

1. Nineteen years ago tonight, at 3:14 AM on November 6, 2007, Casey Tibbs, age 23, was killed in the diner's parking lot when a car without headlights hit her as she walked to her own car in a yellow raincoat. Her mother is Marlene Tibbs, who runs the diner. Casey's son Eli was 2 years old; Marlene raised him.
2. The diner is built on Harlow Crossing, where the old stagecoach road crossed the cattle trail in 1883. The crossing is a thin place. Every November 6 it takes a toll from the road. Its keeper is Mr. Ambrose, a soft-spoken being in a gray coat who appears at the diner every November 6. He is not evil; he keeps the road's ledger.
3. Marlene wears Casey's wristwatch, a small gold watch with a cracked face, stopped at 3:14 since the night Casey died. She has never taken it off.
4. Tonight at 3:14 AM, trucker Ray Kessler, awake for 31 hours, pulls his red refrigerated rig out of the lot onto Highway 51 just as the Dunmores' car, coming back after getting lost, arrives with one headlight broken and the other dim. They collide. The rig jackknifes through the diner's front windows. Four people die: Eli Tibbs (mopping by booth 1), Ray Kessler, Brett Dunmore and Kayla Dunmore. Marlene, at the register, survives.
5. In the first loop, at 3:14, as the headlights filled the window, Marlene gripped Casey's watch and whispered, "Not tonight. Please. Not again." The crossing heard her. Mr. Ambrose granted it: the night repeats from 11:30 PM and will keep repeating until it is settled.
6. The loop ends only when both of these are true at 3:14 AM: first, nobody dies at the diner; second, the road's toll is paid by something freely given back. The truest toll is Casey's watch, given to Mr. Ambrose by Marlene's own hand, which means she lets go of the night Casey died. Ambrose will also accept a hero's offered toll: one cherished memory, forgotten forever (the hero chooses which, and loses it at the end of the game).
7. If only the deaths are prevented and nothing is paid, the loop resets anyway at 3:14. If the toll is paid but people still die, the loop ends with the deaths permanent.
8. Mr. Ambrose chose the heroes as witnesses because each of them, in the first loop, looked him in the eye and said something kind or curious to him. He remembers every loop.
9. The yellow-raincoat woman seen in the lot is an echo of Casey. She cannot speak, but she points: at the watch, at the basement door, at the lot entrance where the crash happens.
10. In the basement is the old crossing stone, a waist-high granite marker from 1883, carved with the words "WHAT THE ROAD TAKES, THE ROAD MAY BE GIVEN. PAY AT THE CROSSING, AND GO ON." Below it are scratched names and years of older tolls. Anything carved or scratched into this stone survives the reset.

## Table Notes

Uncanny and sad rather than gory. The crash is never shown in detail: headlights, a horn, glass, then the jukebox skipping and it is 11:30 again. The heart is grief and letting go. Let players feel clever as they learn the timeline; reward them using knowledge from earlier loops.

Pace: Act 1 is the first loop played straight. Act 2 covers loops two to four, played faster ("you skip ahead to 1:52 when Ray comes in"). Act 3 is the investigation. Act 4 is the final loop. Always say the time on the clock above the counter.

Reset rules, say them plainly when asked: at 3:14 everything and everyone returns to how it was at 11:30 PM. Wounds heal, objects return, phones lose photos and recordings, notes vanish, cars are back where they were, NPCs remember nothing. What survives: the heroes' memories; anything carved into the crossing stone in the basement; any NPC whose hand a hero is holding at 3:14 keeps their memories into the next loop; Mr. Ambrose remembers everything; Marlene feels stronger déjà vu every loop (by loop 3 she says, "Have I told you this already?").

Recurring details: the jukebox song "Same Time Tomorrow" by the Dalton Sisters, playing every time the loop starts; the smell of burnt coffee and cherry pie; the neon sign buzzing "EAT" until 12:10, when the E dies and it reads "AT."

## Characters

- **Deputy Nora Pike**, county deputy on night patrol. Might +2, Agility +1, Wits +1, Spirit +1. HP 14. Gear: service pistol (d6), flashlight, radio, patrol car with keys. Hook: she worked the original crash scene as a rookie 19 years ago and never forgot the yellow raincoat.
- **Abe Okafor**, ER nurse driving home after a double shift. Might +0, Agility +1, Wits +2, Spirit +2. HP 12. Gear: first-aid kit (heals d6, 4 uses per loop), scrubs, energy drink. Hook: he lost a patient yesterday and is wondering if anyone can really be saved.
- **Wanda Briggs**, long-haul trucker, 55. Might +3, Agility +0, Wits +1, Spirit +1. HP 15. Gear: tire thumper (d6), CB radio, her own rig parked by the fuel pumps. Hook: she knows Ray Kessler from the CB and road lore: "Don't stop at Harlow Crossing on the sixth of November."
- **Sky Tran**, paranormal podcaster. Might +0, Agility +2, Wits +2, Spirit +1. HP 11. Gear: phone, voice recorder, EMF meter, notebook. Hook: Sky came to record an episode on the Harlow Crossing legend and has a list of four older deaths at the crossing, all on November 6 (1911, 1946, 1979, 2007).
- **Eli Tibbs**, 17, dishwasher and Marlene's grandson. Might +1, Agility +2, Wits +1, Spirit +1. HP 12. Gear: mop, keys to the diner and basement, phone. Hook: Eli is one of the four who die at 3:14. He barely remembers his mother.

## Opening Scene

11:30 PM. Rain streams down the big front windows. The neon "EAT" sign buzzes red outside. The jukebox drops a record and the Dalton Sisters sing "Same Time Tomorrow." Behind the counter, Marlene Tibbs, 61, pours coffee with a hand that wears a small gold watch. The cook, Hank, scrapes the grill in the kitchen. The clock above the counter reads 11:30. Highway 51 is empty both ways. Each hero is seated, or working, alone.

First: ask each player who they are and why they stopped. Then play the first loop straight, without hinting at the loop until 3:14.

## Spoken Opening

Eleven thirty at night, the sixth of November. The Night Heron Diner sits alone at Harlow Crossing on Highway fifty-one, and rain streams down its big front windows. Outside, the neon sign buzzes red. Eat, it says. Eat.

Inside, it is warm and bright. The air smells of burnt coffee and cherry pie. The jukebox drops a record with a soft click, and the Dalton Sisters begin to sing Same Time Tomorrow.

Behind the counter, Marlene Tibbs, sixty-one, moves along with the pot, topping up cups. On her wrist is a small gold watch. Back in the kitchen, the cook, Hank, scrapes the grill in long, slow strokes.

The clock above the counter reads eleven thirty. Through the glass, Highway fifty-one is empty in both directions, nothing but wet black road and rain.

You came in out of that rain, each of you alone. Some of you sit in a booth, some on a stool, and some of you may be working tonight. The coffee is hot. The night is long.

Why did you stop here tonight, and what do you do?

## Acts

### Act 1: The First Night

**Goal:** live through an ordinary night that turns terrible.

**Scenes (the exact timeline, every loop, unless the heroes change something):**
- **11:30 PM.** Loop starts. Song begins. Rain. Marlene pours coffee.
- **11:42.** Eli drops a tray of glasses in the kitchen. Hank yells, "Third time this week, kid."
- **11:55.** Brett and Kayla Dunmore, newlyweds, arrive arguing about a wrong turn. They order two cheeseburgers and a chocolate shake. Their car's right headlight is smashed (hit a deer two hours ago); the left is dim.
- **12:10 AM.** Power flickers for three seconds. The neon E dies; the sign now reads "AT."
- **12:25.** The police scanner (or Nora's radio) reports a loose cow on Highway 51 at mile 49.
- **12:40.** Mr. Ambrose walks in from the dark, no car, coat dry despite the rain. Sits in booth 6, orders black coffee and cherry pie, pays with a 1921 silver dollar.
- **1:05.** Hank steps out back to smoke and sees a woman in a yellow raincoat at the edge of the lot. She is gone when he looks again.
- **1:20.** The Dunmores leave, heading south. (They get lost and turn around at 2:50.)
- **1:33.** The counter phone rings. Nobody answers on the line; only rain and the same song, faint.
- **1:52.** Ray Kessler parks his red rig in the lot and comes in. Sits on counter stool 3, orders coffee and eggs. Dispatch has told him he will be fired if his load isn't in Dennison by 6 AM.
- **2:15.** Marlene steps out to her car and sits holding her watch, crying for five minutes.
- **2:30.** Ray dozes off at the counter for four minutes. Marlene wakes him gently.
- **2:58.** Ray pays, says "Gotta make time," and goes out to his rig. He sits in the cab doing his logbook.
- **3:10.** Ray starts the engine.
- **3:13.** Ray pulls toward the lot entrance. The Dunmores' car comes north, nearly invisible with no headlights.
- **3:14.** The collision. The rig jackknifes through the front windows. Marlene, at the register, grabs the watch and whispers, "Not tonight. Please. Not again." Then: the jukebox skips, and it is 11:30.

**Clues (first loop):**
- Something is wrong with Mr. Ambrose: dry coat in the rain, a 1921 silver dollar, or Wits DC 13 notices he has no reflection in the window.
- Casey Tibbs's death: a framed newspaper clipping behind the register ("Local Woman Dies at Harlow Crossing, November 7, 2007"), or Hank tells anyone who asks about the yellow raincoat.

**Ends when:** the first reset happens.

### Act 2: Again, and Again

**Goal:** understand the loop and try to stop the crash.

**Scenes:**
- **The Second Loop.** Heroes wake at 11:30 knowing everything. Play out their experiments. Let them predict events to shake NPCs ("The glasses are about to drop").
- **Stopping Ray.** Ways: convince him to sleep in his cab till dawn (Spirit DC 13, or Wanda automatically, citing the CB code), call his dispatcher (Wits DC 13 to find the number on his logbook), disable his rig (Wanda knows the air-brake valve; anyone else Wits DC 16), or Nora arrests or cites him for driving tired.
- **Stopping the Dunmores.** Keep them at the diner past 3:14 (Spirit DC 13, or offer free pie), fix their headlight (Wits DC 13, Wanda has a spare bulb), or warn them of the cow.
- **The Reset Anyway.** Even with no crash, at 3:14 the lights go out, Marlene grips the watch, and the night resets. Heroes now know saving lives is not enough.
- **Loop Shortcuts.** In later loops, let the heroes skip to whatever time they choose.

**Clues:**
- The loop is tied to Marlene: she whispers at 3:14 every loop, heard by anyone near the register, or her watch is stopped at 3:14 (seen at 2:15 in her car or up close at the counter).
- Saving lives alone doesn't end it: the second or third loop with no deaths still resets.

**Ends when:** heroes realize they must learn why the loop happens.

### Act 3: The Crossing

**Goal:** discover the history, the toll, and Mr. Ambrose's role.

**Scenes:**
- **Mr. Ambrose in Booth 6.** He answers questions truthfully but slowly. He says: "The road always takes. Every sixth of November. The lady asked for one more night. I gave it to her. I will give it until the road is paid." He will explain the toll if asked directly.
- **The Basement.** Key from Eli or Marlene, or Agility DC 13 to pick. The crossing stone, carved with the rhyme and scratched names: "Jonas Pell 1911," "Ida Carver 1946," "Mitch Rawlins 1979," "Casey Tibbs 2007." Carving into the stone survives the reset.
- **The Woman in Yellow.** If a hero follows her, she points at Marlene's watch, then at the stone, then walks into the lot where she died and fades.
- **Marlene's Story.** If gently asked (Spirit DC 13, or easy if Eli asks), she tells about Casey: "She was going to her car. I waved from the window. Then the lights. I wear her watch. It stopped when she did."
- **Sky's Research.** Sky's list of older deaths matches the stone.

**Clues:**
- The toll must be something freely given back: Ambrose says it, or the stone's rhyme.
- The watch is the toll: the woman in yellow points at it, or Ambrose says, "What she holds is what keeps her here."

**Ends when:** the heroes understand the toll.

### Act 4: The Last Loop

**Goal:** prevent the crash and pay the toll in the same loop.

**Scenes:**
- **Convincing Marlene.** She doesn't remember the loops. Ways to help her: hold her hand at 3:14 so she remembers next loop; have Eli talk to her (Spirit DC 10); show her the stone with her daughter's name and the heroes' carved messages (Spirit DC 13). She must choose to give the watch to Ambrose herself.
- **Another Toll.** If Marlene refuses, a hero may offer a cherished memory instead. Ambrose accepts, gently, and asks which.
- **3:14.** If both conditions are met: the clock ticks to 3:15. The jukebox plays a new song. Rain stops. Mr. Ambrose tips his hat and walks out into the dawn. If Marlene gave the watch, the woman in yellow waves once from the lot and is gone.

**Ends when:** the clock passes 3:14.

## NPCs

- **Marlene Tibbs**, owner and waitress, 61. *Voice:* warm, tired, calls everyone "hon," hums along with the jukebox. *Wants:* Eli safe; not to lose anyone again. *Knows:* Casey's story. *Secret:* she blames herself for waving from the window. *If threatened:* holds the watch tight and cries.
- **Hank Dobbs**, cook, 58. *Voice:* gruff, short, sarcastic, every sentence ends with "kid." *Knows:* the yellow-raincoat sightings, Casey's story.
- **Ray Kessler**, trucker, 44. *Voice:* slurred, sleepy, overly polite, rubs his eyes. *Wants:* keep his job. *Knows:* his dispatch number is written on his logbook. *If threatened:* apologizes and backs away.
- **Brett and Kayla Dunmore**, newlyweds. *Voice:* Brett is defensive and loud; Kayla is cheerful and laughs nervously. *Wants:* get to her parents' place by morning.
- **Mr. Ambrose**, keeper of the crossing. *Voice:* quiet, courtly, old-fashioned words ("I reckon," "the road is owed"), never in a hurry. *Wants:* the ledger balanced. *Knows:* everything. *Secret:* he grieves every toll. *If threatened:* he is not there when you look again.
- **The Woman in Yellow**, Casey's echo. *Voice:* silent; only gestures.

## Locations

- **The Night Heron Diner.** Counter with 8 stools, 6 booths, jukebox, clock above the counter, kitchen, register, framed clipping.
- **The Parking Lot.** Fuel pumps, Wanda's rig, Ray's red rig, puddles reflecting the neon.
- **The Basement.** Crossing stone, old boxes, a single bulb.
- **Highway 51.** Empty, rain-slick, the long dark north and south.

## Threats

- **The Crash.** Deadly if not prevented.
- **Ray, panicked.** HP 12. Attack +2, d6 (only if cornered).
- **The Fraying.** See Clocks.
- **Mr. Ambrose.** Cannot be harmed; attacks pass through him and the attacker loses d6 HP to cold.

## Clocks

- **The Fraying:** 1) Loop 4: the radio plays only the song. 2) Loop 6: windows show only fog. 3) Loop 8: each hero forgets one real-life detail per loop. 4) Loop 10: the heroes become regulars, part of the diner forever.

## Rewards

- Mr. Ambrose's silver dollar (1921): once, it lets the holder skip a toll on any road.
- Marlene's gratitude: free pie for life.
- Sky's podcast episode becomes a hit.
- Eli's future.

## Endings

- **Triumph:** no deaths, Marlene gives the watch; the night ends, Casey's echo rests, dawn comes.
- **Bittersweet:** no deaths, but a hero pays with a memory; or the loop ends with Ray's death only.
- **Disaster:** the Fraying completes, or the toll is paid with people still dying.
