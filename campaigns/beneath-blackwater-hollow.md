# Beneath Blackwater Hollow

## Pitch

In the fen village of Blackwater Hollow, the well water turned black as ink nineteen days ago, and since then the children have been walking in their sleep, always toward the well. Two nights ago, nine-year-old Pell Rushby climbed down the rope and did not come back. Below the well lies a tomb older than the village, a rival band of treasure-hunters is already inside, and at the very bottom, something is breathing in its sleep.

## DM Truths

1. Nine hundred years ago, the fen-lord Tharn Edevane sang a vast river-spirit to sleep: the Undertow, which the fen-folk called the Old Mother, a black eel the size of a church with a white, lidless eye.
2. Edevane built his tomb over its pool as a lid. His skeleton sits enthroned in the Hush Hall wearing the Hush Crown, a silver circlet with one moonstone that hums his lullaby forever. While it sits on his skull, the Undertow sleeps.
3. The village well, dug 200 years ago, reaches the tomb's Cistern. Villagers once dropped a pebble in each midsummer saying "Sleep sound, Old Mother." Only Old Nan Rushby, Pell's grandmother, remembers why.
4. The Weir Door between the Cistern and the tomb was sealed by three fist-sized stones, Blue, Grey and Green. Nineteen days ago the Gilded Lantern Company pried out the Green one and the seal weakened. Its dreams leaked out as black water and call to anyone who can sing.
5. The Company is four delvers led by Corvin Hask, with a Merrowgate buyer paying 2,000 gold for the crown. A fifth, Dobbin Fyfe, died in the Hall of Reeds trap. They are camped in the Drowned Chapel, stuck because they cannot work out the safe path over the Stepping Stones.
6. Pell Rushby is alive. The dreaming drew her down because her grandmother sang her the lullaby every night. She walked past the sleeping Company, crossed the Stepping Stones by instinct, and sits at the foot of Edevane's throne in the Hush Hall, singing the lullaby over and over in a trance. Her singing helps keep the Undertow asleep.
7. The lullaby, which Pell sings and Old Nan can teach: "Hush now, water, still now, deep, / the lord of reeds will guard your sleep. / Count the stones and close the door, / and wake no more, and wake no more."
8. To fully reseal the tomb: the crown must stay on Edevane's skull, someone must sing the lullaby once through in the Hush Hall, and the Green stone must go back into the Weir Door on the way out. Mags Tolliver of the Lantern Company carries the Green stone in her belt pouch. When all three are done, the well runs clear by morning.
9. If the crown is lifted, the Undertow wakes in stages (the Waking clock) and finally rises up the well to flood the village.

## Table Notes

A classic delve. Describe each room in two or three sentences, name its exits, and ask what they do. Acts run 45 to 60 minutes each. Build dread slowly; the Undertow is never fully seen.

Content care: Dobbin's body is pale and still, never gory. Pell is never harmed on screen.

Recurring details: wet stone and peat smoke; dripping water that sounds like counting; the lullaby humming louder the deeper they go. When the Undertow stirs, all water ripples at once and lamps gutter blue.

## Characters

- **Hollis Rushby**, Pell's uncle, a broad-shouldered eel-fisher. Might +3, Agility +1, Wits +0, Spirit +1. HP 16. Gear: eel-spear (d8), 50 feet of rope, lantern. Hook: Pell is his niece; he promised his sister Edda he would bring her home.
- **Sister Maudry Fenn**, a young wandering priestess of the Lantern Saint. Might +0, Agility +1, Wits +1, Spirit +3. HP 12. Gear: iron censer on a chain (d6), 3 flasks of holy water (heal d6 each). Hook: her order's records mention "the lord of reeds who sings the deep to sleep"; she wants to know if it is true.
- **Tamsin Crale**, a sharp-eyed tomb-sapper who used to work with Corvin Hask. Might +1, Agility +3, Wits +2, Spirit +0. HP 12. Gear: two daggers (d6), lockpicks, crowbar, chalk. Hook: Corvin left her for dead in a collapsed mine a year ago; she wants payback or an apology.
- **Ambrose Quill**, a nervous antiquarian from Merrowgate. Might +0, Agility +0, Wits +3, Spirit +1. HP 10. Gear: sword-cane (d6), notebook of old fen script. Hook: he reads old fen script, and suspects his employer, Lord Saffery Vane, is Corvin's buyer.
- **Bryn Tallow**, a village shepherd-girl with a sling and no fear. Might +1, Agility +2, Wits +1, Spirit +2. HP 13. Gear: sling (d6), 20 smooth stones, crook (d6). Hook: Pell is her best friend, and Bryn was meant to be watching her the night she vanished.

## Opening Scene

Dusk in Blackwater Hollow, twenty stilt-houses in the reeds under low peat smoke. The village stands around the well with lanterns. The water below is black and gives back no reflection. Pell's mother, Edda Rushby, is weeping. Old Nan Rushby, bent and white-haired, grips the well's edge and says: "You'll need the song. Don't go down without the song." Reeve Gammon says the Lantern Company went down three weeks ago and never came up. The first choice: talk to Old Nan and the villagers, or climb straight down.

## Acts

### Act 1: The Well and the Weir Door

**Goal:** Learn what they can in the village, then get down the well and through the Weir Door.

**Scenes:**
- **The village square.** Old Nan will sing the whole lullaby if asked kindly (no roll). If rushed, she sings only the first two lines; a Spirit roll DC 10 gets the rest. She also says: "The lord of reeds counted the stones. Heron, frog and otter carry you. The eel takes you." Reeve Gammon says the Lantern Company asked about "the old king under the well." Bryn or any child says the sleepwalkers all hum the same tune.
- **The Well Shaft.** Sixty feet down: rope for thirty, then carved handholds. Agility DC 10, or fall into the water for d6. A ledge at forty feet bears Corvin's chalk mark: "C.H. — 3 down, 2 to go."
- **The Cistern.** Vaulted brick, waist-deep black water, four stone eel statues. Exits: the Weir Door north, a drain-crawl west to the Ossuary Shelf. Three cave eels bite anyone who splashes loudly.
- **The Ossuary Shelf (side path).** A sixty-foot crawl ends at a shelf of old fen-folk skulls, each with a pebble in its mouth, and a heron-carved scroll-case. Its poem in old fen script (Ambrose, or Wits DC 16) reads: "Heron, then frog, then never the eel; otter will carry, and reed will kneel." Also here: a bone flute (see Rewards).
- **The Weir Door.** A round stone lock-gate, ajar. Three sockets: Blue stone top, Grey left, right socket empty with fresh crowbar marks. Carved above in old fen script: "Three to hold her. Two to warn her. One to wake her." Wits DC 13 or Ambrose: the empty socket matches a missing green stone (flakes of green on the rim).

**Clues:** The lullaby and its words (Old Nan; or Pell's humming heard in Act 3). The safe stones rhyme (Old Nan's saying; or the scroll on the Ossuary Shelf; or Wren Quillon in Act 2). The Green stone was taken by the Company (crowbar marks; or Wren tells them Mags has it).

**Ends when:** The heroes pass through the Weir Door into the tomb proper.

### Act 2: The Hall of Reeds and the Mouth of Edevane

**Goal:** Survive the first trap, answer the Mouth's riddles, and meet the rival party.

**Scenes:**
- **The Hall of Reeds.** A corridor of waist-high stone reeds over large floor slabs. Dobbin Fyfe lies face-down halfway along with a lantern and 12 gold. Trap: every third slab fires scalding steam from the reeds, d8, Agility DC 13 halves. Wits DC 13 spots it, or notices "one, two, THREE" scratched by the door. Counting aloud gets everyone through. Exits: the Mouth of Edevane at the far end, a side arch to the Bone Garden.
- **The Bone Garden (side path).** Servants' bones arranged as flowers on the walls; two Reedmen guard it. A stone basin holds 40 silver coins and a moonstone ring. If the heroes hum the lullaby, the Reedmen bow and step aside.
- **The Mouth of Edevane.** A huge stone face whose mouth is a door. It asks three riddles; each right answer opens it a third. Each wrong answer sprays cold water, d6 to everyone. Three wrong answers flood the room: swim back to the Hall of Reeds (Might DC 13 or d6) and the Mouth resets. Wits DC 13 gives a hint for any riddle.
 - Riddle one: "The more of me the fen-lord had, the less he saw. I came each night and left each dawn." Answer: darkness (or night). Hint: "You carry the thing that kills me."
 - Riddle two: "I wear a crown of white but have no head. My feet are in the water but my crown stays dry. The fen-folk cut me for their roofs." Answer: a reed (thatch is close enough). Hint: "You walked past a hall full of my stone brothers."
 - Riddle three: "I keep the Old Mother sleeping. I have no hands, yet I hold her. No rope, yet I bind her. Any child can carry me in her mouth." Answer: a song, or the lullaby. Singing it aloud also counts. Hint: "Your grandmother gave you one."
- **The Drowned Chapel.** Half-flooded, rotting pews, a bronze bell underwater, the Company camped on the altar. Exits: back through the Mouth, or on to the Stepping Stones.
- **Meeting the Gilded Lantern Company.** Corvin Hask greets the heroes with a crossbow and a smile. He offers a 60-40 split of "the old king's treasure" for help crossing the Stepping Stones, and denies touching the Weir Door. If Tamsin is present, he goes pale. Wren quietly begs a hero to get her out. Brother Ashe mutters that the water talks to him. Mags keeps a hand on her belt pouch.

**Clues:** Pell came this way (a small muddy footprint on the altar step, Wits DC 10; or Wren saw "a little girl walk past singing" two nights ago and thought she was dreaming). Mags has the Green stone (Wren tells; or Agility DC 16 to pick her pouch; or Tamsin notes her old trick of keeping loot on her belt). The crown keeps the thing asleep (Brother Ashe has heard it in dreams; or Ambrose reads the chapel altar: "While the lord wears the moon, the Mother sleeps").

**Ends when:** The heroes cross the Stepping Stones, with or without the Company.

### Act 3: The Stepping Stones and the Deep Stair

**Goal:** Cross the Black Pool and follow Pell's singing down.

**Scenes:**
- **The Stepping Stones.** Eighty feet of black water, a blue glow beyond, twelve stones each carved with a fen animal. Spoken in order from the near shore: heron, eel, frog, eel, otter, frog, eel, reed, otter, eel, heron, reed. The rule from the rhyme: step on heron, frog, otter and reed stones; never step on eel stones. Hopping an eel stone is Agility DC 10. Stepping on an eel stone sinks it: the hero drops into the water among the cave eels, d6 damage, and must swim to the next stone (Might DC 13).
- **The Weeping Gallery.** A ledge over a shaft; the humming is clearly a child now. Carvings show Edevane singing to a giant eel, then sitting on a throne, then the eel curled asleep below. Ambrose or Wits DC 13 reads the caption: "Lift the moon and she lifts her head." Exits: the Deep Stair down, and a cracked side door to the Treasury.
- **The Treasury (side path).** A small vault guarded by a needle lock (Agility DC 16, or Tamsin at DC 13, or a crowbar and Might DC 16 with noise that advances the Waking clock one step). Holds 300 gold, a silver horn and Edevane's sword (see Rewards).
- **The Deep Stair.** 99 slick spiral steps. Halfway down, Corvin and Mags (if with them) try to push past and race for the crown; Wren may side with the heroes.

**Clues:** Lifting the crown wakes the thing (the Weeping Gallery carvings; or Brother Ashe's dreams). Pell is singing below (heard by everyone; Spirit DC 10 picks out the words of the lullaby).

**Ends when:** The heroes reach the bottom of the stair and enter the Hush Hall.

### Act 4: The Hush Hall

**Goal:** Save Pell, stop Corvin, and decide what to do about the sleeping thing.

**Scenes:**
- **The Hush Hall.** A round hall around a pit of still black water sixty feet across. A stone bridge leads to an island throne where Edevane's skeleton sits in rotted green cloth, wearing the Hush Crown, its moonstone glowing faintly. At his feet sits Pell Rushby in her nightdress, eyes open but unseeing, singing the lullaby. Deep under the water, something white and round as a cartwheel slowly opens and closes: an eye.
- **The race for the crown.** If Corvin got ahead, he is crossing the bridge as they enter. If he lifts the crown, start the Waking clock.
- **Waking Pell.** Pulling her away is safe only if someone else keeps singing; otherwise the Waking clock advances one step. Calling her name wakes her (Spirit DC 13; automatic for Bryn or Hollis).
- **The resealing.** With the crown on the skull, one hero sings the lullaby all the way through (Spirit DC 10, or automatic if they learned it from Old Nan). The eye closes. The humming steadies. Then on the way out, the Green stone must go back in the Weir Door.
- **The temptation.** The crown is worth 2,000 gold. If the heroes take it, the Undertow wakes. A hero wearing it can sing it back to sleep, but they would have to stay here forever, as Edevane did.

**Ends when:** The Undertow sleeps again, or rises.

## NPCs

- **Old Nan Rushby**, Pell's grandmother, ninety-one. *Voice:* a thin, sing-song quaver; she breaks into humming mid-sentence. *Wants:* Pell home and the old ways remembered. *Knows:* the full lullaby, the stone rhyme, the pebble custom. *Secret:* her grandmother went down the well to sing the last time the water darkened, and came back. *If threatened:* just sings louder.
- **Reeve Gammon**, headman. *Voice:* gruff, clears his throat before every sentence. *Knows:* the Company asked about "the old king." *Secret:* he took a 10-gold bribe to let them go down without telling anyone. *If threatened:* confesses immediately.
- **Edda Rushby**, Pell's mother. *Voice:* hoarse from crying, answers with questions. *Knows:* Pell hummed the lullaby in her sleep for a week.
- **Corvin Hask**, leader of the Gilded Lantern Company. *Voice:* smooth and friendly, calls everyone "friend," laughs softly before bad news. *Wants:* the Hush Crown and the 2,000 gold. *Knows:* the Company took the Green stone; he thinks the sleeping thing is a story. *Secret:* his buyer is Lord Saffery Vane of Merrowgate; Corvin owes him 800 gold. *If threatened:* bargains, then shoots, then runs. Surrenders at 4 HP.
- **Mags Tolliver**, sapper. *Voice:* flat, bored, one-word answers. *Knows:* she has the Green stone in her belt pouch; she kept it because it is pretty. *If threatened:* hands over the stone for 50 gold, or if shown the carvings proving the danger.
- **Brother Ashe**, disgraced priest. *Voice:* a trembling whisper; repeats the last word others say. *Wants:* the voice in the water to stop. *Knows:* the Undertow is real; he has dreamed its eye every night. *If threatened:* drops to his knees and begs.
- **Wren Quillon**, the Company's young scout, seventeen. *Voice:* fast and breathless, apologizes constantly. *Wants:* to go home alive. *Knows:* Mags has the stone, Pell walked past singing, the stone rhyme half-remembered ("Heron, frog, something, never eel"). *If threatened:* switches sides at once.
- **The Mouth of Edevane**, the riddling door. *Voice:* very slow and deep, every word its own sentence.
- **Tharn Edevane**, dead fen-lord. If a hero wears the crown or sings to him, his voice answers once in their head: *Voice:* tired, kind, like a grandfather at bedtime. He says: "Put it back, child. Someone must always sing."

## Locations

- **Blackwater Hollow.** Twenty stilt-houses, the well in the square, the Drowned Duck alehouse (ale 2 copper), a reed-thatch shrine. Peat smoke and frog song.
- **The tomb, in order going down:** Well Shaft, Cistern (side crawl west to the Ossuary Shelf), Weir Door, Hall of Reeds (side arch to the Bone Garden), Mouth of Edevane, Drowned Chapel, Stepping Stones, Weeping Gallery (side door to the Treasury), Deep Stair, Hush Hall. Each room's details are in its scene in the Acts.

## Threats

- **Cave eels** (3 in the Cistern, 4 under the Stepping Stones). HP 5 each. Attack +3, d6 bite. Only attack those in the water; flee when one dies or they are fed.
- **Reedmen** (2). Skeletal servants wrapped in reeds. HP 10 each. Attack +4, d6 with bronze sickles. Special: reform after one hour unless their skulls get a pebble placed in the mouth. Stand aside for the lullaby or a pebble in a skull.
- **Corvin Hask.** HP 14. Attack +4, crossbow d8 or rapier d6. Surrenders at 4 HP.
- **Mags Tolliver.** HP 12. Attack +3, hammer d8. Gives up if Corvin falls.
- **Wren Quillon.** HP 9. Shortbow +3, d6. Never attacks the heroes.
- **The Undertow.** Not a fight; a disaster to escape. It cannot be harmed. Each round awake, a coil sweeps the walkway (Agility DC 16 or d10 and knocked into the water).

## Clocks

- **The Waking (starts only if the crown is lifted, or if the heroes make huge noise like smashing the Treasury):** 1) All water in the tomb ripples, lamps gutter blue. 2) The eye opens fully; Pell stops singing and screams; the bridge cracks. 3) A coil the width of a cart breaks the surface; the Deep Stair starts to flood from below. 4) The Undertow rises up the well; by dawn the village is under water. Setting the crown back and singing at step 1 or 2 stops it. At step 3, it also takes a Spirit DC 16 song.
- **The Lantern Company (runs while the heroes dawdle in Acts 2 and 3):** 1) Corvin guesses the first three stones correctly. 2) Brother Ashe drowns in the Black Pool trying to cross (unless helped). 3) Corvin and Mags reach the Deep Stair. 4) Corvin lifts the crown; start the Waking.
- **The Village (between acts):** 1) Two more children sleepwalk to the well. 2) The water rises to ten feet below the rim. 3) Old Nan climbs down to sing and gets stuck on the ledge.

## Rewards

- Dobbin's pack: 12 gold and a lantern.
- Bone Garden basin: 40 silver and a moonstone ring worth 25 gold.
- The bone flute from the Ossuary Shelf: playing the lullaby on it calms any animal or Reedman automatically, once per act.
- The Treasury: 300 gold; a silver horn worth 60 gold that, blown underwater, drives off eels; and Edevane's sword, Stillwater, a heavy blade (d8) that glows blue near the Undertow and gives +1 to Spirit rolls to resist fear.
- Village gratitude: 100 gold from Reeve Gammon's chest, free lodging for life, and Old Nan's blessing.
- If the heroes take the crown: 2,000 gold from Lord Saffery Vane, and a flooded village on their conscience.

## Endings

- **Triumph:** Pell is rescued, the crown stays on Edevane's skull, someone sings the lullaby, and the Green stone goes back into the Weir Door. By morning the well runs clear. The village revives the pebble custom.
- **Bittersweet:** Pell is saved but the Green stone is lost or the song is botched. The Undertow sleeps, but lightly. Someone must go down every midsummer to sing, and one hero (or Old Nan, or Pell herself when grown) takes up that duty. Or a hero puts on the crown to stop the waking, and stays below to sing forever.
- **Disaster:** The crown leaves the tomb. The Undertow rises up the well that night and Blackwater Hollow vanishes under a black lake. The heroes escape with whoever they carried. Each hero hears the lullaby in their dreams forever.

## Off the Map

- **People:** Ottilie Marsh the alewife, Fennick Gale the ferryman, Jory Sedge the reed-cutter, Widow Bramley, Hal Brine the peddler, Vane's agent Mr. Thimble.
- **Places:** the Drowned Duck alehouse, Sedgemoor Causeway, the Heron Stones, Merrowgate (two days east).
- **If they leave the story:** Run the Village and Lantern Company clocks. Within two days Corvin lifts the crown and the Waking runs to its end. Old Nan sends a messenger begging them back.
