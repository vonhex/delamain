# The Goblin King's Toll

## Pitch

The village of Puddlewick sits on the far side of the River Dimble, and the only bridge is the old stone one. Five days ago a goblin calling himself King Grizzlewick the Magnificent, First and Only, set up a toll gate on it. On Monday the toll was a joke. On Tuesday, one sock. Today nobody knows, and the village Harvest Fair is in two days.

## DM Truths

1. The goblins of the Muddlebank Warren, eleven of them, lived happily in the old drainage tunnels under Miller Brambleby's field for a hundred years. Three weeks ago Miller Brambleby built a new millpond dam upstream, and the water flooded their warren. Nobody in the village knew goblins lived there.
2. The goblins moved under the old bridge, which is cold and damp. Princess Pipsy, the king's six-year-old daughter, caught the Grumbles: a goblin sickness where you go grey, stop giggling and cannot stop sighing. Goblins who have the Grumbles too long turn into stone garden ornaments. Pipsy has until Sunday morning (see her clock).
3. The goblin healer, Granny Gobbet, knows the cure from her old recipe-song: "A joke that's good, a sock that's warm, a song to sing against the storm, a secret told, a feather fine, and a friend who says: you're welcome, you're mine." The tolls are the ingredients, one per day. The goblins thought a toll was the polite way to ask.
4. Tolls so far: Monday, a joke (only one was funny enough; the cart-driver Old Tobble's joke about the duck). Tuesday, one sock (they have 31 socks; none warm enough). Wednesday, a song (the baker Mrs. Crumbcake sang, and it worked). Thursday, a secret (they collected 12 secrets, which they keep in jam jars and do not understand). Today, Friday, the toll is "a feather from the finest bird in Puddlewick." Tomorrow, Saturday, the toll will be "a friend." The goblins have no idea how to get that one.
5. The finest bird in Puddlewick is Duchess, the Mayor's prize-winning white goose, who has won the Harvest Fair four years running and bites anyone who comes near.
6. The warmest sock in Puddlewick is the one Grandma Wooley is knitting for the Fair's Longest Sock contest. It is already eleven feet long.
7. Mayor Plumtree is furious about the toll and has hired Sir Reginald Pompworth, a self-styled goblin-catcher, for 20 gold. Sir Reginald has never caught a goblin. He is terrified of them. His plan is a giant net on the bridge on Saturday morning.
8. The final ingredient only works if a human says "you're welcome, you're mine" to a goblin and means it: a real offer of friendship. If the village invites the goblins to the Harvest Fair, Pipsy is cured on the spot.

## Table Notes

This is a cozy comedy for kids and adults together. Almost every problem can be solved with jokes, kindness, cleverness or a song; fighting should be rare, silly and painless (goblins "fall over" and complain, nobody is hurt). Reward funny ideas generously. Pace: about 45 minutes per act, with breaks for goblin silliness.

Content care: Pipsy's illness is sad but never scary; she is grey and sighing, not in pain. If kids worry, Granny Gobbet says there is plenty of time if everyone helps.

Recurring details: goblins say "Oi!" to start every sentence; the bridge toll gate is a broken garden gate painted gold with a sign that says TOLE; the smell of fresh bread from Mrs. Crumbcake's bakery; Duchess the goose's furious honking, which can be heard from anywhere in the village.

## Characters

- **Hazel Thimble**, a cheerful baker's apprentice. Might +1, Agility +1, Wits +1, Spirit +2. HP 12. Gear: rolling pin (d6), a basket of 6 honey buns, flour. Hook: Mrs. Crumbcake sent Hazel across the bridge to fetch Fair supplies; Hazel has to get back by Saturday.
- **Dame Hortensia Bluebell**, a retired knight with a creaky knee and a big heart. Might +2, Agility +0, Wits +1, Spirit +2. HP 15. Gear: old sword (d8, she prefers not to use it), shield, a flask of peppermint tea. Hook: she and Sir Reginald trained together forty years ago, and he still owes her 3 silver.
- **Fidget Quicksilver**, a juggler and fair performer. Might +0, Agility +3, Wits +1, Spirit +1. HP 11. Gear: juggling clubs (d6), a squeaky trumpet, a book of 100 jokes (only 4 are good). Hook: Fidget is booked to perform at the Fair, and can't get across the bridge.
- **Olwen Mossy**, a young hedge-witch with a talking toad. Might +0, Agility +1, Wits +2, Spirit +2. HP 11. Gear: willow wand (sparkles, d6 "zap" that only tickles), herb pouch, Burdock the toad (grumpy, can taste lies). Hook: Olwen has heard of the Grumbles and knows it is serious.
- **Barnaby Hobb**, the miller's son, big and gentle. Might +3, Agility +0, Wits +0, Spirit +1. HP 16. Gear: a sack of flour (very heavy), a shovel (d6), a wooden whistle. Hook: his father built the dam, and Barnaby has a secret feeling it is somehow his family's fault.
- **Tilly Fernsby**, a sharp-eyed eleven-year-old who wants to be a detective. Might +0, Agility +2, Wits +3, Spirit +0. HP 10. Gear: magnifying glass, notebook, slingshot (d6), pocket full of marbles. Hook: Tilly has been trying to solve "the Case of the Goblin Toll" since Monday.

## Opening Scene

A sunny autumn morning on the road to Puddlewick. Apple trees, fat bumblebees, the River Dimble glittering. The heroes reach the old stone bridge to find a gold-painted garden gate across it and a sign: TOLE. Behind the gate, on a throne made from an upturned wheelbarrow, sits a goblin the size of a large dog, wearing a teapot lid as a crown. He blows a whistle. "Oi! Halt! By order of King Grizzlewick the Magnificent, First and Only, all who cross must pay TODAY'S TOLL!" He unrolls a scroll. "Today's toll is: a feather from the finest bird in Puddlewick!" The heroes have no feather. What do they do?

## Acts

### Act 1: The Bridge

**Goal:** Get across the bridge and find out what is going on.

**Scenes:**
- **The toll gate.** King Grizzlewick and two guards, Snibbit (tall, nervous) and Gubbs (short, eats everything). They will not let anyone pass without the toll, but they are easily confused. Ways across: offer any feather and argue it is fine (Spirit DC 13; Grizzlewick declares "close enough" if it is very fancy); trick them (Wits DC 13; for example, "the finest bird is behind you!"); bribe Gubbs with food (honey buns work automatically, but he only opens the gate for one person); or swim (Might DC 10, very wet, Hazel's flour turns to paste).
- **The jam jars.** Behind the throne sit 12 jam jars with paper lids, each labelled "SEKRIT." Grizzlewick guards them proudly. If asked, he explains he collected secrets yesterday "for the royal medicine." Tilly or Wits DC 10: some have names on them, and one is labelled "Mayor Plumtree."
- **The goblin camp.** Under the bridge: damp, cold, nine more goblins huddled under a moth-eaten quilt. A small grey goblin girl sighs every few seconds. If the heroes look under the bridge (Agility DC 10 to climb down, or just ask), they see her. Granny Gobbet shoos them away unless they are polite.

**Clues:** The tolls are for medicine (Grizzlewick lets it slip about the jam jars; or Granny Gobbet tells polite visitors). Someone is sick (the sighing heard from under the bridge, Wits DC 10; or Snibbit blurts out "for the princess!" when nervous).

**Ends when:** The heroes cross into Puddlewick, or are invited under the bridge.

### Act 2: Puddlewick in a Pickle

**Goal:** Learn the village side of the story and find the feather.

**Scenes:**
- **The village square.** Mayor Plumtree, round and red-faced, is announcing that "the goblin menace" will be dealt with on Saturday morning by "the famous Sir Reginald Pompworth!" Sir Reginald bows, his helmet falls over his eyes. Villagers grumble: they had to tell their secrets, which was embarrassing.
- **Mrs. Crumbcake's bakery.** She says the goblins were very polite when she sang. "The little king cried, I think." She will give the heroes a free bun each and the advice: "Ask them what they need, dears. Nobody's asked them."
- **Miller Brambleby's dam.** Upstream, a brand-new dam and a wide millpond. At the edge, half-sunk tunnel entrances with tiny wooden doors floating in the water, and a soggy sign: "Muddlebank Warren, Pleese Nock." Barnaby recognizes them with a gulp. Miller Brambleby is cross, but not mean. He did not know. If the heroes tell him, he says he can open a sluice to drain the north tunnels, but only if the Mayor agrees.
- **Duchess the goose.** In the Mayor's garden, a huge white goose with a blue ribbon. She honks, she hisses, she chases. Ways to get a feather: chase her (Agility DC 16, d6 bites that only hurt pride); bribe her with bread (she loves Mrs. Crumbcake's buns: Spirit DC 10 with a bun); sing to her (she falls asleep, Spirit DC 13); or ask the Mayor's gardener, Hob Pottle, who has a whole jar of molted Duchess feathers (he will give one for a good joke).
- **The Case of the Missing Pies.** Three pies vanished from Mrs. Crumbcake's windowsill. The village blames goblins. Three suspects, and the heroes can question them: Gubbs the goblin says "Snibbit took them." Snibbit says "I didn't take them." Hob Pottle the gardener says "Gubbs is telling the truth." Burdock the toad tastes the air and croaks that exactly one of the three is telling the truth. Answer: Gubbs and Snibbit contradict each other, so one of them is truthful; if Gubbs were truthful, Hob would be too, making two. So Snibbit is the truthful one, and Gubbs and Hob both lied. Caught lying, Hob confesses: his own dog, Biscuit, stole the pies, and he blamed the goblins so he wouldn't get in trouble. Tilly or Wits DC 13 works it out; anyone who just asks Burdock directly who lied gets a grumpy croak at Gubbs and Hob. The pies are found under the Mayor's shed.

**Clues:** The goblins were flooded out (the dam's sunken doors; or Granny Gobbet). Pipsy has the Grumbles (Olwen knows the signs; or Granny Gobbet explains). The cure recipe-song (Granny Gobbet sings it to polite heroes; or the lyrics are written on the back of the toll scroll, Wits DC 10). The Mayor's secret (in the jam jar: "I am scared of geese, even my own").

**Ends when:** The heroes bring the feather to the bridge, or are invited into the goblin court.

### Act 3: The Court of King Grizzlewick

**Goal:** Learn the whole truth and win the goblins' trust.

**Scenes:**
- **The royal audience.** The goblins hold court under the bridge on upturned buckets. Royal etiquette: you must bow, say "Oi" before speaking, and compliment the king's teapot lid. Forgetting etiquette makes the goblins giggle, which is the first giggle Pipsy has managed in days.
- **Princess Pipsy.** Grey, small, sighing. She asks each hero, "Do you have a friend?" She has never had a human friend. Making her smile (a joke, a trick, a juggling act; Spirit DC 13, or automatic for a genuinely funny joke from a player) gives her one day back on her clock.
- **Granny Gobbet's riddle.** Before she will trust them with the last ingredient, Granny Gobbet asks a riddle: "The more of me you give away, the more of me you have. What am I?" Answer: friendship (or kindness, or love; she accepts any of those). Wits DC 13 for a hint: "You've got some in your basket right now, if you share it."
- **The Royal Feast.** If the heroes are polite and helpful, the goblins throw them a feast: mud pies (actual mud), acorn soup (actually tasty), and "pond pudding." Eating politely earns the honorary title of Royal Friend. Refusing earns sighs.
- **The worst toll.** Grizzlewick admits the last toll is "a friend" and he's worried. "Oi. Nobody wants to be friends with a goblin. Everyone says so." This is the heart of the story.

**Clues:** The last ingredient is a real friendship offer (the recipe-song; or Granny Gobbet explains). Sir Reginald's net (Snibbit spies on the village and saw it; or the heroes overheard the Mayor in Act 2).

**Ends when:** The heroes promise to help the goblins, or decide to help the Mayor.

### Act 4: The Harvest Fair

**Goal:** Stop the net, change the village's mind, and cure Pipsy.

**Scenes:**
- **Saturday dawn, Sir Reginald's net.** A huge fishing net rigged on the bridge, with Sir Reginald hiding in a barrel. Ways to deal with it: warn the goblins; untie it (Agility DC 13); talk Sir Reginald out of it (Spirit DC 13, or automatic if Dame Hortensia reminds him of the 3 silver he owes her and the time he cried at a squirrel); or let it fall on him instead (very funny).
- **Convincing the Mayor.** Mayor Plumtree will not invite the goblins unless he is persuaded. Things that work: the truth about the dam; the pies solved; a goblin apology for the tolls; promising to keep his goose secret; or a passionate speech (Spirit DC 13, with +2 for each of the other things the heroes have done).
- **The Longest Sock.** Grandma Wooley's eleven-foot sock is the warmest sock in the village, and it's needed for the cure. She'll trade it for help winning the contest: it needs to reach twelve feet. Ideas: borrow Duchess's ribbon to tie on the end; have the goblins knit with their 31 socks; or stretch it (Might DC 13, risky; on a fail it unravels by a foot).
- **The Fair.** Contests: the Longest Sock, the Biggest Marrow, Goose of the Year (Duchess versus a surprise goblin entry, a duck named Admiral Wobble), and the Talent Show. If the goblins are invited, they enter everything and are terrible and delightful.
- **The cure.** Pipsy is brought up into the sunshine. With the joke, the warm sock, a song, a secret, the feather, and someone saying "You're welcome, you're mine," Granny Gobbet stirs it all into a teacup. Pipsy turns green again and giggles so hard she hiccups.

**Ends when:** Pipsy is cured, or the Fair ends at sunset.

## NPCs

- **King Grizzlewick the Magnificent, First and Only**, the goblin king. *Voice:* a big pompous voice from a small body, "Oi!" before every sentence, refers to himself as "We." *Wants:* Pipsy well. *Knows:* the recipe, the toll schedule. *Secret:* he is very lonely and has never been invited anywhere. *If threatened:* bursts into tears and hides behind his throne.
- **Princess Pipsy**, his daughter, six. *Voice:* tiny, sighing between words; when cured, an endless giggle. *Wants:* a friend.
- **Granny Gobbet**, goblin healer. *Voice:* creaky and bossy, calls everyone "duckie." *Knows:* the cure, the riddle. *If threatened:* whacks with her spoon (d6, only hurts pride).
- **Snibbit**, tall nervous goblin guard. *Voice:* high-pitched, stammering, blurts secrets.
- **Gubbs**, short goblin guard. *Voice:* mouth always full, mumbles, says "Snack?" a lot.
- **Mayor Plumtree**, mayor of Puddlewick. *Voice:* booming, pompous, uses long words wrong ("This is an outrage-ification!"). *Wants:* a perfect Harvest Fair. *Secret:* he is scared of geese, including his own. *If threatened:* calls for Sir Reginald.
- **Sir Reginald Pompworth**, goblin-catcher. *Voice:* posh, quavering, says "Tally-ho!" when scared. *Wants:* 20 gold and not to touch a goblin. *Secret:* he has never caught anything but a cold.
- **Mrs. Crumbcake**, baker. *Voice:* warm, sing-song, calls everyone "dear." *Knows:* the goblins were kind.
- **Miller Brambleby**, owner of the dam. *Voice:* gruff, says "Hmph" before speaking, softens quickly. *Knows:* how to open the sluice.
- **Grandma Wooley**, champion knitter. *Voice:* very slow, very deaf, says "Eh?" a lot. *Wants:* to win the Longest Sock.
- **Hob Pottle**, the Mayor's gardener. *Voice:* sheepish and mumbling. *Secret:* his dog Biscuit stole the pies.

## Locations

- **The old stone bridge.** Gold-painted garden gate, wheelbarrow throne, TOLE sign, twelve jam jars. Underneath: the damp goblin camp with a moth-eaten quilt.
- **Puddlewick village square.** A maypole, a well, bunting going up for the Fair.
- **Mrs. Crumbcake's bakery.** Warm, smells of bread; three empty pie dishes on the windowsill.
- **The Mayor's garden.** Duchess's goose-house, a shed with stolen pies under it, Hob Pottle's jar of feathers.
- **Miller Brambleby's dam.** New wood, wide millpond, little floating goblin doors.
- **The Muddlebank Warren.** Flooded tunnels; if drained, cozy rooms with tiny furniture.
- **The Fair field.** Tents, contests, a stage for the Talent Show.

## Threats

- **Duchess the goose.** HP 8. Attack +4, d6 bite (only pride damage). Calms down for buns or singing.
- **Sir Reginald Pompworth.** HP 10. Attack +1, d6 with a wooden sword. Runs away if a goblin says "Boo."
- **The goblins** (11). HP 4 each. Attack +2, d6 "nibble." Surrender if anyone offers food.
- **Real danger:** none. Fights end with someone falling over and laughing.

## Clocks

- **Pipsy's Grumbles:** 1) Pipsy stops talking. 2) Her ears go grey and stiff. 3) She can't get out of bed. 4) She turns into a garden ornament (the cure still works, slower, and she'll wake up in spring). The clock starts at zero on Friday morning and moves every morning and evening (Friday evening, Saturday morning, Saturday evening, Sunday morning); each time she smiles it moves back one step.
- **The Mayor's Plans:** 1) Sir Reginald is hired. 2) The net goes up Friday night. 3) The net falls Saturday dawn. 4) The goblins are put in a cart and sent "far away" unless someone stops it.

## Rewards

- 10 silver from Mrs. Crumbcake for the found pies.
- Duchess feathers, if gathered: a pillow fit for a queen, 2 silver.
- Royal Friend of the Goblin King: a teapot-lid medal; any goblin anywhere will help the heroes once.
- The Mayor's thanks: 20 gold (Sir Reginald's fee) if the heroes solve it instead of him.
- Grandma Wooley's spare socks: everyone gets warm feet forever.

## Endings

- **Triumph:** The goblins are invited to the Fair, Pipsy is cured, the miller drains the north tunnels and builds a little goblin door in the dam, and King Grizzlewick knights each hero "Sir or Dame Oi." Every year after, the goblins run the Fair's toll gate, and the toll is always a hug.
- **Bittersweet:** Pipsy is cured, but the village still doesn't want goblins nearby. The goblins move to the woods. They visit the heroes once a year at the Fair, wearing disguises (fake mustaches) that fool nobody.
- **Disaster (still gentle):** The net falls, the goblins are carted away, and Pipsy becomes a garden ornament. The heroes must find the goblins in the next valley and carry the ingredients there. The story ends with Pipsy waking up in spring, still grey around the ears, and asking, "Are you my friend now?"

## Off the Map

- **People:** Farmer Tuppence, the Widdle sisters, Constable Plod, Little Ned Hollyhock, Madame Marigold the fortune teller, Mr. Bumblesnout the beekeeper.
- **Places:** the Snoring Badger inn, Dimble Woods, Gobbler's Hill, Buttercup Meadow, the market town of Lower Pudding.
